# G2 — phase 2b: the two kernels that carry the matching stage

**Date:** 2026-09-08. Branch `rust-core`, from `fd879b7` (the head of task G1).
Design references: D §6.2 (the hash grid), §6.3 (buffers), §6.4 (batch formation and the software
pipeline), §6.5 (kernel semantics), §6.6 (expected speedups), §6.7 (CPU/GPU agreement), §6.8
(operational concerns), §7 (determinism and the two numerics knobs), §10.2 (tolerances), §10.4
(test layers), §12 (phasing). Experiments E2 (the CPU profile), E5 (`f32` in the ICP), E7 (wgpu on
Metal), C2 (the ICP and its chaotic ladders), G1 (the executor boundary and the device).
Machine: Apple M2 Pro, 10 CPU cores (6P+4E), 16-core GPU, 16 GB, one Metal adapter, no software
fallback.

**Verdict: both kernels are correct, the run they produce is indistinguishable from the CPU's, and
the stage they belong to is 1.0×.**

R §5.2's coarse score is **bit-identical to the CPU executor's on 2 934 052 of 2 934 122
hypotheses** across the eight development sets, and the other 70 — a rate of 2.4e-5 — are one probe
point of sixty apart, which is E7 §5.1's FMA residue landing exactly where it was predicted to and
nowhere else. R §7's rung is inside D §10.2's stage-1 rotation row on seven sets of eight and inside
its translation row, read at the cloud, on all eight; its stage-2 tail is a few candidates per set,
and a control run separates the ones the *ladder* would send anywhere from the one that is the
kernel's arithmetic.

End to end, `--backend gpu` and `--backend cpu` produce **the same candidates, the same accepted
joins, the same groups and the same `tools/evaluate.py` scores on all seven collections**, with
every shared join's score identical to the bit; two GPU runs of each are byte-identical. There is
no difference to diagnose against R §13's seed spread, because there is none.

And the matching stage is **1.0×** on ten threads (1.21× on one). The device is busy 6.35 s of
`synthetic_20`'s 15.4 s stage and idle for the rest, while ten rayon threads take turns blocking on
it. The kernels measure 1.6–4.2× in isolation; D §6.4's block scheduler — "CPU threads prepare, one
GPU thread submits; double-buffered" — is what would let the stage see that, and it is not built.
So `Backend::Auto` keeps the CPU and prints the measured reason, and `GpuExecutor::AUTO_ELIGIBLE`
flips when the scheduler lands rather than when a kernel does.

## 2. R §5.2's coarse score: a count, and what that buys

The kernel returns the **integer agreement count** per hypothesis. The score is the CPU executor's
own `f64::from(agree) / n_points` — a division, in `f64`, on the host — because step C1 measured
that `k · (1/60)` is not `k / 60`. So two counts that agree give two scores that agree *bit for
bit*, and the cross-check is a comparison of integers rather than an argument about the last bit of
a mean.

Measured over the eight development sets, four pairs each, every hypothesis of every pair:

| set | hypotheses | scores differing | worst | over one probe point |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| terracotta | 114 463 | **0** | 0 | 0 |
| pot_A | 988 382 | 27 | 1/60 | 0 |
| pot_B | 742 581 | 37 | 1/60 | 0 |
| pot_C | 265 556 | 1 | 1/60 | 0 |
| pot_G | 114 588 | 1 | 1/60 | 0 |
| pot_H | 205 068 | 2 | 1/60 | 0 |
| synthetic_20 | 487 147 | 2 | 1/60 | 0 |
| slab | 16 337 | **0** | 0 | 0 |
| **total** | **2 934 122** | **70** | **1/60** | **0** |

**70 hypotheses of 2.93 M, a rate of 2.4e-5, and every one of them by exactly one probe point of
sixty.** That is E7 §5.1's residue arriving where it was predicted to: the transformed query point
is `R·p + τ` and Metal contracts that into FMAs, so a probe point sitting within a ULP of the
correspondence radius — or a shell normal whose dot product sits within a ULP of 0.7 — falls on the
other side of the test. It cannot be more than one point, because one query decides one count, and
the "over one probe point" column is what checks that rather than assuming it.

R §5.4's re-score is the same kernel over a different point set — B's whole breakline subset at
`sc.stage1` rather than sixty points at `sc.coarse` — and shows the same thing: **3 differing
scores of 6 110** across the eight sets (one each on pot_A, pot_C and pot_H), each by exactly one
point of the subset (2.4e-3, 1.9e-3 and 2.8e-3, which are `1/410`, `1/518` and `1/362`).

D §10.2's `coarse` row is `≤ 1/60 + 1e-6` **and bit-exact against the reference's own array**; the
port meets the second against Python and the GPU meets the first against the port. That is D §6.7's
distinction exactly, and it is why the row is stated in probe points.

## 3. R §7's rung: the frame, and the 6×6

### 3.1 The shift, and the correction D §7 does not mention

E5 measured `f32` point loops in the scans' own coordinates at **9.8 t** for the median stage-2
pose on pot G. The scans sit 100–150 units from the origin, where `f32` resolves 1e-5, and a
fragment's own extent is tens of units — so D §7's last row prescribes the fix: *"Shrinking
coordinates for an `f32` path means translating both clouds by −c — a rigid change of frame R §7 is
equivariant under — not re-parameterising the Jacobian alone."*

The host does exactly that, in `f64`: the source by its own centroid `c_s`, the target by its own
`c_t`, with the initial pose carried across as `T' = [R | R·c_s + τ − c_t]` and the answer carried
back. **The equivariance R §7 has is not the equivariance the sentence claims**, and the difference
is one line of the kernel:

* **R §5.4's estimator is equivariant.** Umeyama's translation is `mean_q − R·mean_p`, and shifting
  the clouds shifts the two means with them, so the update computed in the shifted frame is already
  the conjugate of the world one. Nothing to correct.
* **R §5.6's estimator is not.** The point-to-plane step reads its translation off a linearisation
  about the origin *of whatever frame it is in*. The linear system is an exact reparameterisation —
  `ω` is unchanged and `τ_world = τ' − ω × c`, which follows from `ω·(c × n) = n·(ω × c)` — but the
  finite update is not: composing `p ↦ R p + τ'` in the shifted frame is conjugate to composing
  `p ↦ R p + τ' + (R − I − ω̂)c` in the world frame. That residue is `O(|ω|²·|c|)`, and it is
  precisely D §7's `Assembly::Centred`, which task C2 measured at **0.044 t at the median and
  0.61 t at p90** on terracotta — outside D §10.2's 0.01 t row.

So the kernel computes the residue and adds it. `τ_update = τ' + (R − I − ω̂)·c_t`, which is the
world composition exactly, and which is better conditioned than the equivalent `R c − c + τ_world`
because `R − I − ω̂` is formed from `O(1)` entries before it ever meets a 150-unit `c` — a
cancellation argument, not a measurement: the other form was never built, so there is nothing to
quote against it.

### 3.2 Equilibration: the one place the kernel departs from `icp::solve_ldlt`

`JTJ`'s rotational block is `(p × n)ᵀ(p × n)` and its translational block is `nᵀn`, so with the
clouds shifted to their centroids the two still differ by `|p|² ≈ 10³` — a condition number bought
entirely by the choice of units, not by the geometry. In `f64` that costs three of sixteen digits
and nobody notices. In `f32` it costs three of seven.

Scaling by `D = diag(1/√A_kk)` and solving `(D A D) y = D b`, `x = D y`, is an exact
reparameterisation. Measured over terracotta's first two pairs, four stage-2 rungs each:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| pose deviation from the CPU rung, p90 | 6.5e-2 ° | **7.1e-4 °** |
| displacement, p90 | 4.8e-3 t | **7.8e-5 t** |
| pose deviation, p50 | 3.5e-4 ° | 3.3e-4 ° |
| pose deviation, worst | 2.1 ° | 2.1 ° |

The median barely moves and p90 falls by ninety times, which is the signature of a conditioning fix
rather than a change of answer; the worst case does not move at all, because that candidate's
trouble is elsewhere (§3.4). It **does** change which permutation Eigen's rule picks — after
scaling every diagonal entry is 1 and `maxCoeff` keeps the first — and that is a real departure
from `sherd_core::matching::icp::solve_ldlt`, made deliberately: the two orders factorise the same
matrix, D §10.2's tolerances are what this path is held to, and the version that lands closer to
the `f64` answer is the version to run. The doc comment on `solve_ldlt` in `icp.wgsl` says so.

### 3.4 The question the task asked: does the centred `f32` solve suffice?

> *"measure the pose deviation from the f64 CPU rung on every fixture pair and say whether the
> centred system suffices or a f64-emulated solve is needed for the fine rungs"*

**For R §5.4's rungs it suffices, with room.** Worst under 5e-4° on five sets of eight and under
D §10.2's 0.05° on seven, p90 ≤ 8e-5° on all eight, and the translation inside D §10.2's row at the
cloud on all eight. Nothing here wants
more precision.

**For R §5.6's fine rungs the answer is "yes at p90, no at the worst case, and a `f64`-emulated
solve would not be the fix."** Three pieces of evidence, in the order they were measured:

1. **The solve was the second-biggest term, and equilibration already took it.** Scaling the 6×6 by
   its own diagonal moved p90 from 6.5e-2° to 7.1e-4° (§3.2) — ninety times, for eleven lines of
   WGSL. That is the size of the gain an exact solve could have offered; a `f64`-emulated LDLT would
   now be chasing what is left of it.
2. **The convergence test is not the term either, and that was worth checking.** R §7 stops on
   `|Δfitness| < 1e-6 ∧ |Δrmse| < 1e-6`. The fitness half is exact — `count / n_source` in `f32` is
   exact for counts under 2²⁴, so the test is really "the correspondence count did not change" —
   but a plain `f32` sum of the squared error carries ≈ 1.6e-6 of relative error, *above* the RMSE
   threshold, so the rung stops as soon as the pose moves less than the summation noise. It does
   stop early: 30 of pot_A's 40 stage-2 candidates end at a different iteration, by 16 of 30 at
   p90. And it costs almost nothing — pot_A's worst stage-2 deviation is 1.1e-2°. A rung that has
   converged does not move in its last sixteen iterations.
3. **What is left is the point loop, and it is `f32` by design.** The `JTJ` accumulation is
   55 additions deep after the lane stride and the tree, at ≈ 3e-6 relative; through an equilibrated
   6×6 that is ≈ 3e-7 radians per iteration, and thirty iterations of it is the 3e-4° median the
   table shows. The tail is not that error growing — it is a candidate whose correspondence set
   changes at some iteration and whose ladder then goes somewhere else, and no precision in the
   *solve* prevents a different nearest neighbour.

So: the answer to the fine rungs is not a wider solve, it is **not running them in `f32` at all**,
or accepting the tail. This task accepts it, on the evidence of §5: the tail changes no join on any
development set. If a later phase needs the fine rungs bit-tighter, the cheap move is to leave R
§5.6's last two rungs on the CPU — they are ten candidates a pair, the crossover table says the
device barely wins there anyway, and `icp::MIN_CANDIDATES` already sends most of them to the CPU in
a real run.

### 4.2 The stage, on a real collection

`synthetic_20`, 20 fragments, 190 pairs, previews and meshes off, caches warm. Matching-stage
seconds:

| | CPU | GPU | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--threads 1` | 103.37 | **85.39** | **1.21×** |
| ten threads, median of five | 15.76 | 15.92 | 0.99× |
| ten threads, best of five | 15.36 | 14.70 | 1.04× |

Single-threaded the kernels show through: 1.21× on the whole stage, which is what Amdahl's law
gives for two kernels that are 1.6–4.2× on somewhat over half of it. On ten threads they do not
show at all.

The counters say why, and they are the reason the executor keeps them. From the single-threaded
run, where a `poll` waits for this thread's submission and nothing else:

```
coarse: 380 calls, 192 on device, 188 delegated, 194 dispatches, 4333.7 ms gpu, 1651249020 items
icp:    1148 calls, 134 on device, 1014 delegated, 134 dispatches, 2019.7 ms gpu, 621760 items
gpu busy 6.35 s in total
```

**The device is busy 6.35 s of a stage the ten-core CPU finishes in 15.4 s.** It is not the limit;
it is idle for nine of those fifteen seconds while ten rayon threads take turns blocking on it.
D §6.4 describes the shape that would fix it — "CPU threads prepare, one GPU thread submits;
double-buffered" — and phase 2b did not build it, because it is a change to `pipeline`'s scheduler
and not to a kernel.

Per-kernel rates from the same run:

* **coarse** — 1.651 G point-queries in 4.334 s: **2.63 ns/query**, against E7 §5's 9.0–9.5 ns on a
  cloud where every query hits. The difference is R §5.2's bounding-box reject (§6.2), which retires
  three quarters of the probe before it reaches the grid.
* **icp** — 621 760 candidate-iterations in 2.020 s: **3.25 µs per candidate-iteration**, at 15 ms
  per dispatch.

### 4.3 The projection for 170 fragments, and what it is worth

170 fragments is 14 365 pairs. `synthetic_20`'s 190 pairs cost 6.35 s of device time, i.e.
**33.4 ms of GPU per pair**; scaled, 14 365 pairs are **8.0 minutes of GPU time** if the device were
kept busy end to end. The CPU's own measured stage is 15.4 s / 190 = 81 ms of ten-core wall per
pair → **19.4 minutes**, which brackets D §6.6's 17-minute CPU projection from the other side.

**Both of these are projections and neither has been run**: nothing above 27 fragments was executed
in this task, `synthetic_20`'s pairs are smaller than `mixed_all`'s, and the 8.0 minutes assumes a
scheduler that does not exist. Read it as *the device could do the matching stage's kernel work in
about eight minutes*, and the 19.4 as *the CPU does the whole stage in about twenty* — the gap
between them, not either number, is what D §6.4's block scheduler is worth.

**D §6.6's assumption, re-derived.** Its GPU column rests on "≈ 0.5–1 G bounded NN queries/s",
which is 1–2 ns/query; E7 measured 9.0–9.5 ns and G1 12.2–17.9 ns, and D §6.6 has carried a warning
since that it is 5–10× optimistic. Those measurements are of a saturated kernel on a cloud where
**every query hits**. R §5.2's own workload is not that: three quarters of the probe is outside the
breakline's box, and with the reject in the kernel (§6.2) the coarse score runs at **2.63 ns/query**
— 0.38 G queries/s, within 1.3–2.6× of the assumption rather than 5–10× outside it. Both numbers
are right; they are of different workloads, and the design's row should say which one it means. Its
per-pair GPU total (0.08 s for a mid-size pair) brackets the 33.4 ms measured here on
`synthetic_20`'s smaller pairs, so the column is the right order of magnitude for the first time.
What it does **not** yet describe is a stage: it assumes "the batches keep the GPU busy while the
CPU prepares the next block", and §4.2 is what happens when they do not.

**Determinism (D §10.4 layer 4).** Two `--backend gpu` runs of each set, into separate trees,
compared with G1's own byte comparator: **7, 11, 12, 10, 10, 14 and 23 files, all identical**, with
only the wall-clock fields of `report.json` and `report.md` softened. The kernels are deterministic
per backend, which is what D §7 asks of them; they are not bit-identical to the CPU's, which is what
D §6.7 says they cannot be.

**How much of each run was actually the device.** The `gpu busy` column is the summed per-dispatch
wall time over the pool, so on ten threads it over-counts (each thread's poll now waits on its own
submission, but a busy queue still delays it); §4.2's single-threaded 6.35 s is the honest figure
for `synthetic_20`. What the column does say truthfully is *which* sets reached the device at all:
terracotta at 0.09 s barely did — its rungs are under `icp::MIN_CANDIDATES` (§6.3) and only the
coarse score ran — while pot_A's 5.78 s is a collection where both kernels were used throughout.
Even terracotta's coarse-only run reproduces the CPU's seven joins exactly, which is the weaker half
of the gate; pot_A, pot_B, pot_H and synthetic_20 are the halves that carry it.

## 6. Six things that went wrong, or were nearly got wrong

### 6.1 A batch of one, and the refine stage that gave the whole gain back

The first `--backend gpu` terracotta run was **1.55× slower than the CPU one end to end**, and the
matching stage was not where it went: `refine` was 1.89 s against 0.74 s. R §9's refinement calls
`icp_rung` with **one** candidate, and D §6.4 puts one workgroup on one candidate — one workgroup on
a sixteen-core GPU, against ten CPU cores with SIMD. The crossover table of §4.1 says that costs
0.3×, and the fix is a threshold, not a kernel. It is worth stating as a rule rather than as an
incident: *the boundary that makes a GPU path expressible* — one `Executor` method, any batch size —
*is exactly the boundary that lets a caller hand it a batch it must refuse.*

### 6.2 The bounding box, and a crossover measured on the wrong data

The first coarse kernel had no early-out: D §6.2's grid answers an empty neighbourhood in 27 cell
probes, and that looked like the fast path. The crossover table agreed — 1.55× at 2.4 M queries —
and the pipeline did not: the matching stage came out **1.04× slower on the GPU**, with the kernel
at 13.8 ns/query against the ten-core CPU's 12.6 ns of wall.

The table had been measured on a cloud where **every query hits**. R §5.2's own workload is the
opposite: E2 §4 measured three quarters of the probe landing outside the breakline's bounding box,
where `PointTree::nearest_below` retires it in three comparisons and the grid was paying
twenty-seven probes. Adding the same box reject to the kernel took it to **2.63 ns/query** and the
crossover to 3.4×.

**The task asked for E2's mask semantics to be "reproduced or shown redundant on the GPU", and the
answer is both, in different senses.** *Semantically* it is redundant on either executor:
`NearMask::may_be_near` returns `false` only when no breakline point is within the radius, so a
caller that falls through on `true` computes exactly what it computes without the mask — the kernel
running without it **is** computing the masked semantics, and the coarse table of §2 is the
measurement of that (a masked CPU batch and an unmasked kernel agree on 2 934 052 of 2 934 122
hypotheses, and the 70 are the FMA residue and not the mask). *Operationally* it is not redundant at
all, and that is the part the first kernel got wrong.

Two lessons, and the second is the useful one. A kernel benchmark on synthetic data measures the
kernel; it does not measure the stage, and the two can disagree in *direction*. And E2's near mask
is a **speed** structure whose value is not "it skips a tree descent" but "it skips work at all",
so a GPU port that drops it has to replace it with something. Here the box did; the second, dilated-cell half of E2's filter did not go in, because its
cell index is `⌊(q − lo)·(1/cell)⌋` and an `f32` rounding of that lands in the neighbouring cell,
whose dilated block is not a superset of the right one. Making it safe means dilating by two and
giving most of the rejection back. What that leaves on the table is the quarter of the probe that
is inside the box and still finds nothing.

### 6.3 A cross-check that compared the CPU with itself

The first eight-set sweep came back with every terracotta ICP row `ok` at a deviation of exactly
zero. It was not: **all 24 of the four pairs' rungs were under `icp::MIN_CANDIDATES × MIN_WORK`** —
terracotta's breakline cloud is under 400 points — so the executor had sent every one of them to the
CPU and the harness had compared the CPU with itself, printing a zero nobody measured.

This is task G1's own failure mode arrived at from the other side. G1 built the `delegated` label
because a GPU executor with no kernels would otherwise report agreement; G2 rebuilt the same trap
out of a *performance* threshold. `gpu-check` now forces the kernels on by default and **every row
that compares a kernel says how many of its calls reached the device** — `[device]`, `[N of M calls
on the device]`, or `delegated — every call was the CPU's, so this row compares nothing`.

### 6.4 `pose_gap` reports 3.6e-2 degrees between a pose and itself

The first ICP rows showed 223 of 500 stage-1 poses "differing" by up to 3.6e-2 degrees while the two
executors were still both the CPU. The poses were bit-identical; the *metric* was not zero.

`sherd_parity::stages::pose_gap` reads the angle off `trace(Rᵀ R') = 1 + 2 cos θ`, and
`Σ R²ᵢⱼ` is 3 only for an exactly orthonormal `R`. R §5.1's hypothesis rotations are built from
breakline frames that came out of an `f32` cloud and are orthonormal to about 1e-7, which is
3.6e-2 degrees of spurious angle — **72 % of D §10.2's 0.05° stage-1 row, on a pose compared with
itself.**

`gpu-check` uses the Frobenius form instead, `θ = 2 asin(‖R − R'‖_F / 2√2)`, which is the same angle
for a true rotation and exactly zero for identical bits. **This is a finding about the verification
harness, not about the GPU**, and it is left here rather than acted on: D §10.2's rows were
calibrated with the trace form against the reference's poses, where both sides carry the same floor,
and re-deriving them is not phase 2b's to do. It is worth a line in a later verification pass.

### 6.5 Kahan compensation, folded away

`search`'s squared-error sum carries a Kahan compensation, added to bring the RMSE noise under
R §7's 1e-6 convergence threshold (§3.4 item 2). **On this device it does nothing.** Every row of
`gpu-check --set input/sfspp/pot_A --stage icp` came back identical to the last printed digit with
it added, including `icp s1 rmse`'s p50 of 9.758e-8 — exactly the quantity it would have moved.
`(next - err2) - term` is algebraically zero and Metal's fast math is entitled to say so.

E7 §3's finding does not cover this: it measured that the compiler leaves a fixed-order addition
*tree* bit-identical to its Rust mirror, i.e. that it does not **reassociate**. Applying an algebraic
identity is a different licence and this compiler takes it. The three lines stay, because they are
correct WGSL and a Vulkan or DX12 backend compiled without fast math gets the tighter sum; the
comment in `icp.wgsl` says they are inert here.

### 6.6 The self-test's ratio is not the stage's ratio

D §6.8's `Backend::Auto` rule takes the GPU when "the measured throughput beats the CPU by ≥ 1.5×",
and the thing it measures is the self-test: E7 §5's bounded-NN kernel, on an idle device, at 3–6×.
That number is real, it is reproduced on every run, and **it is not the matching stage's**, which is
1.0× (§4.2). A rule that read it would switch the default to a backend that is not faster.

So `GpuExecutor::HAS_KERNELS` (true — the kernels exist and `--backend gpu` runs them) is now
separate from `GpuExecutor::AUTO_ELIGIBLE` (false — and the constant carries the measurement that
makes it false). `Selection::decide` reads the second. The flag flips when D §6.4's block scheduler
lands, not when a kernel does.

## 7. What is not done, and what could not be tested here

* **D §6.4's block scheduler.** "CPU threads prepare, one GPU thread submits; double-buffered" is
  the difference between §4.2's 6.35 s of device time and the 15.4 s stage it sits inside. It is a
  change to `pipeline`'s pair scheduling, not to a kernel, and it is the single thing that would
  make `AUTO_ELIGIBLE` true. Nothing in this task's boundary changes for it: the batches, the
  thresholds and the counters are what it would schedule.
* **E2's dilated near mask on the device** (§6.2). The bounding box is in; the cell mask is not, and
  the note says what makes it unsafe in `f32` rather than leaving it as an omission.
* **`bounded_distance` and `inside`** are phase 2c's, delegate, and say so in every table.
* **`cone_cast`** is still not in the trait; D §6.1 puts it with the kernel that needs it and no
  kernel needs it yet.
* **The fragment slot table has no consumer.** `sherd_gpu::slots::SlotTable` is D §6.3's resident
  set and it is still what G1 left: tested arithmetic with nothing dispatching against it. These two
  kernels upload their grid and their clouds per batch, which is what makes the coarse kernel's
  155 ms on pot_A's four pairs partly upload; a scheduler that kept a fragment resident would take
  that back, and that is the same scheduler as the bullet above.
* **`Chunking` still has no consumer**, for the same reason it had none in G1 — no batch of a
  development set reaches the 128 MB binding cap. Both kernels compute the split (`coarse.rs`'s two
  caps, `icp.rs`'s correspondence array) and neither has ever needed more than one chunk.
* **Everything but Metal.** lavapipe is Linux, WARP is Windows, and this machine has one integrated
  Metal adapter and no software fallback (E7 §6). The `gpu-check` CI job covers those rows
  `continue-on-error`; the two kernels have run on exactly one device.
* **Nothing above 27 fragments was run.** The 170-fragment figures in §4.3 are projections from
  measured per-pair costs and are labelled as such.
