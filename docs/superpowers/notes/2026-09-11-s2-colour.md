Apple M2 Pro, 10 cores (6P + 4E), 16 GB, macOS 24.6.0, rustc 1.97.0, `--release`, Python 3.12 in

**The five sentences.** *Colour is now a feature and not a curiosity*: every fragment carries the

## 1. What a fragment now carries

### 1.1 The split, and the one moment it can be taken

R §3.4 labels **faces of the working mesh**. The colours are on the vertices of the **file**. The two
never meet: R §3.3 decimates and Taubin-smooths the file into the working mesh and drops the vertex
colours on the way, so by the time a face has a label there is no colour left to split, and while the
colours are still in memory there is no label yet.

`Fragment::from_mesh_file_named` is the one function where both exist. It already kept the file's
vertices for M1 §4's `lab_mean`; it now keeps them in a `RawColours` carrier until R §3.4 has written
`seg.labels`, calls `features::split_colour` on that line, and drops the carrier immediately:

```rust
// Task S2, here and nowhere else: the file's colours are still in memory and R §3.4's
// labels have just been written, which is the one moment both exist.
let (shell_colour, frac_colour) =
    features::split_colour(&raw_colours, &geom.centroids, &seg.labels);
drop(raw_colours);
```

The bridge from a file vertex to a working-mesh label is the **nearest labelled face centroid**,
found through the project's own `PointTree` and not a scan, with the sRGB → Lab conversion memoised
per distinct RGB triple — which is why the terracotta's 2 166 251 coloured vertices, carrying
24 191–31 978 distinct triples each, cost 0.33 s and not a minute. The alternative bridge, a barycentric projection onto the nearest
face, buys accuracy the 4×4×4 histogram cannot use and pays for it on the largest fragment in the
collection (`V094_frag_007`, 1 220 258 coloured vertices).

**A file with no colours carries nothing and pays nothing.** `RawColours::of` returns an empty carrier
when the mesh has no colour array or one whose length does not match the vertices, `split_colour`
returns `(None, None)` for it, and `ColourStats::of` returns `None` for an empty side. Absent is
`None` at every level and never a zero: a fragment R §3.4 gave no fracture face reads as
**unavailable**, not as black.

### 1.2 Three statements per side

`ColourStats` is a Lab **mean** (where the colour sits), a per-channel Lab **MAD** (how far it
spreads — median absolute deviation, not a standard deviation, because a single painted band would
own a standard deviation) and a fixed **4×4×4 = 64-bin Lab histogram** (what shape the colour cloud
has). The mean says *what colour*; the histogram says *what pattern*; §2.3 shows they answer
different questions and that the better one is not the one the brief expected.

Two distances are defined on it and both ship:

* `delta_e` — CIE76 between the two means. Plain Euclidean distance in Lab, which is what "colour
  difference" means to the eye to within about one unit.
* `hist_distance` — **total variation** between the two normalised histograms: `0` for two identical
  shapes, `1` for two that share no bin. Total variation and not χ² because the counts are vertex
  counts and two fragments of one vessel differ in size by two orders of magnitude on these sets;
  normalising first reads the shape and nothing about how many vertices the scanner wrote. Its known
  weakness is the bin edge — two colours a just-noticeable difference apart on opposite sides of one
  count as fully different — which is why the mean is reported beside it and not replaced by it.

### 1.3 The cache

`CACHE_VERSION` 6 → 7. The split cannot be recomputed from a cached fragment, because the vertices it
is measured on are the file's and R §3.3 has decimated them away; an older cache is therefore refused
and its fragment read from the scan again, transparently and once. What that costs, cold cache
against cold cache, on the four standing sets at seed 0:

| set | colour it carries | preprocess S1 → S2 | whole run S1 → S2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `terracotta` | 4 museum scans, 2 166 251 coloured vertices, 24 191–31 978 distinct triples each | 2.19 → **2.54 s** (+16 %) | 3.83 → 4.20 s |
| `synthetic_20` | 20 fragments, a photograph each | 7.98 → **8.63 s** (+8 %) | 27.5 → 29.4 s |
| `pot_H` | 11 fragments, one flat grey | 0.60 → **0.55 s** (−8 %) | 11.5 → 11.9 s |
| `pot_A` | 8 fragments, no colour array | 1.45 → **1.50 s** (+3 %) | 10.1 → 10.5 s |

Both columns are one cold-cache run at seed 0 on an otherwise idle machine, so the two rows without
real colour — `pot_H`'s single grey costs nothing to split and `pot_A` carries nothing to split at
all — are the size of the run-to-run spread, in both directions. The price of the split is the two
rows above them: **8–16 % of preprocessing on a collection that actually carries a photograph**, paid
once because the result is in the cache.

### 2.3 What the table shows — five readings, two of them against expectation

1. **The clay body clears the bar and nothing geometric comes close.** `frac_lab_a` is 0.985 and
   0.990; the best geometric feature on the same two collections is `shell_radius` at 0.777 and
   `axis_residual` at 0.770/0.736, and M1 §4's best anywhere was 0.740. This is the first feature on
   this project that audit §D.2 would let veto.
2. **The `a` axis carries it, and pooling the channels throws it away.** `frac_lab_a` is 0.985 but
   `frac_lab_L` is 0.707 and the pooled CIE76 `frac_delta_e` is **0.935** — worse than its own best
   channel. Lightness is what a fracture surface's shading does to a photograph; `a` is what the clay's
   iron does. A rule that measures the clay body should measure the `a` axis, not a Euclidean distance
   that averages it with a noisy `L`.
3. **The brief expected the painted skin to be the unreliable half; the histogram says the opposite,
   and the mean agrees with the brief.** `094_bemalte_schuessel` is painted, so the skin's *mean*
   lightness varies inside the object exactly as predicted — `shell_lab_L` is **0.478**, below chance.
   But the skin's *histogram* is the single best statement in the table (**1.000** / 0.995), because
   the decoration is a per-vessel palette: two sherds of one painted bowl share the same set of
   colours in different proportions, and total variation reads the set. The prediction was right about
   the mean and wrong about the shape.
4. **The controls behave, in two different ways.** `pot_A` and `pot_B` carry no vertex colours at all
   and every colour row of theirs is **unavailable** — absent, not neutral. `pot_C`–`pot_H` (and
   therefore 147 of `mixed_all`'s 164 fragments) do carry a colour: **one flat grey**, Lab
   (77.70, 0.00, 0.00), one distinct RGB triple per fragment. So their colour is *available and
   constant*, every pairwise distance is 0 to a rounding error and every AUC is exactly **0.500**.
   Both readings are correct and neither can veto anything. But they are **not** interchangeable for
   a rule that reads colour as *positive* evidence: an absent colour produces no `ColourAgreement`
   at all, while a constant one produces a pair distance of zero, which is indistinguishable from
   two sherds of one vessel agreeing perfectly. §10 turns that into the sharpest warning this note
   has for S3.
5. **Two vessels of one clay are not separated by colour, and the note says so before the museum
   does.** `synthetic_170` is a 158-fragment vessel plus a 6-fragment intruder, both from the same
   generator and the same pingsdorf body: their clay-body medians are Lab (54.24, 27.79, 32.09) and
   (50.61, 26.15, 30.20), a difference smaller than one vessel's own per-fragment tint, and every
   colour AUC on the set is **0.44–0.46** — *below* chance. This is the case a real collection from
   one kiln presents, and it is why §10 does not recommend colour as a general-purpose veto. Colour
   separates *these* three vessels because they are three different pots photographed separately,
   not because colour separates pots.

## 3. The scale a colour is measured on: `COLOUR_MAD_FLOOR`

A `k·MAD` consensus divides by the group's own spread, which is right for a wall thickness — nothing
outside the collection knows how thick a wall should be — and wrong for a Lab channel, which comes
with an absolute scale: one unit is roughly the smallest difference a person sees.

The failure is not hypothetical; it is a line out of a real run, made on the tree between S2.1 and
S2.2 before the floor existed:

```
V012_frag_000 is 11.8 MAD from object 0's frac_lab_a (3.3299 against 3.6224)
```

A group of `V012` sherds agreed on their clay body's `a` to a MAD of **0.0248** Lab units, so a
member differing by **0.2925** — a quarter of one just-noticeable difference, invisible to anyone —
sat at **11.8 MAD** and was thrown out of its own object.

`FeatureKey::mad_floor()` therefore returns `COLOUR_MAD_FLOOR = 3.0` for the nine Lab channels and
**0.0 for every geometric feature**, which leaves `Consensus::mads` exactly the test it was. The
number is chosen on the three single-object controls: it has to clear the widest spread a collection
that is *one vessel* shows, because everything above it is read as a second vessel.

| control | what it is | widest deviation of `frac_lab_a` from the collection median |
|---|---|---:|
| `input/test_fragments_1` | 4 real museum scans, one object | **0.39** |
| `synthetic_pingsdorf_20` | 20 synthetic, one vessel, per-fragment tint | **3.32** |
| `synthetic_pingsdorf_60` | 56 synthetic, one vessel, per-fragment tint | **6.61** |

With `--object-mad` at its default 3 the effective limit is `3 × 3 = 9` Lab units. That is above all
three, so no single-vessel collection is touched by the floor; and `V049`'s clay body sits **11.4**
units from `V012`'s on the `a` axis, so a real mismatch is still outside it. `V094` is 4.2 from
`V012` and 7.3 from `V049` and would **not** be caught — the floor buys the extreme pair and not the
middle ones, which is the honest description of a rule with one number in it.

## 4. What letting colour demote would cost, and what the floor buys

`--object-demote frac_lab_a,shell_lab_a`, CPU, seeds 0–4, everything else at the shipped defaults.
Three arms, all under `output/s2/arm/`; the third is the tree **between S2.1 and S2.2**, before
`COLOUR_MAD_FLOOR` existed, and it is the reason the constant does.

| arm | set | confirmed joins, seeds 0–4 | total | correct | **false** | demotions |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| shipped (`--object-demote` empty) | `synthetic_mix3_24` | 11 / 7 / 6 / 7 / 8 | **39** | 39 | **0** | **0** |
| shipped | `synthetic_pingsdorf_20` | 5 / 9 / 9 / 8 / 11 | **42** | 41 | **0** | **0** |
| colour demotes, **with** the floor (committed) | `synthetic_mix3_24` | 11 / 7 / 6 / 7 / 8 | **39** | 39 | **0** | **0** |
| colour demotes, **with** the floor (committed) | `synthetic_pingsdorf_20` | 5 / 9 / 9 / 8 / 11 | **42** | 41 | **0** | **0** |
| colour demotes, **no floor** (pre-S2.2) | `synthetic_mix3_24` | 7 / 4 / 4 / 5 / 8 | **28** | 28 | **0** | 4 / 3 / 2 / 2 / 0 |
| colour demotes, **no floor** (pre-S2.2) | `synthetic_pingsdorf_20` | 4 / 6 / 7 / 4 / 8 | **29** | 29 | **0** | 1 / 3 / 2 / 4 / 3 |

Three things to read off it.

**Without the floor the rule is a wrecking ball.** Eleven correct confirmed joins lost on the coloured
set, thirteen on the monochrome one, **and not one false join removed**. Every demotion is a sherd
refused by its own vessel over a colour difference no eye could see: seed 0 threw `V012_frag_000` out
of `V012` three times over 0.2925 Lab units, and seeds 1–3 threw `V049_frag_003` out of `V049` seven
times over **0.42** units on `shell_lab_a`. That is a `k·MAD` rule meeting a group that agrees too
well, and it is a property of the *scale*, not of the feature.

**With the floor the rule never fires at all.** Re-measured on the committed binary
(`output/s2/arm/floor2`): zero demotions on both sets at all five seeds, and the confirmed joins are
the shipped arm's **pair for pair** — the two sets of `(A, B)` names compare equal at every seed.
§3's arithmetic says why: `3 × 3 = 9` Lab units is above every deviation a single-vessel collection
produces, and `synthetic_mix3_24` never offers a cross-object pair for the rule to catch (§5), so
there is nothing left in range.

**Which is exactly why colour still ships off the shortlist.** M1 §4's reason was "no feature clears
audit §D.2's bar". S2's is different and worth writing down: *the feature that clears the bar is,
after the floor that makes it safe, measured to do nothing at all on every set this project can
measure it on.* A default that never fires is not a default; `--object-demote frac_lab_a` is the flag
for the first collection that can test it, `objects::COLOUR_MAD_FLOOR` is what makes it safe to try,
and this table is what it costs on either side of that constant. The three clay-body channels are in
`FeatureKey::REPORTED`, so `## Objects` prints what colour each assembled vessel is and which of its
members do not match it, and the `demotes` column stays empty.

## 6. What shipped

| | shipped | behind a flag | not shipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| **features** | `shell_colour` and `frac_colour` on every fragment, cached at version 7 | — | — |
| **objects** | six split channels in `FeatureKey::ALL`; three clay-body channels in `REPORTED`, printed in `## Objects`; `COLOUR_MAD_FLOOR = 3.0` | `--object-demote frac_lab_a[,shell_lab_a]` — measured, inert with the floor (§4) | any colour on the default demote shortlist |
| **tiers** | `Evidence::colour` on every accepted candidate: `frac_delta_e` + `shell_hist` | — | any rule that reads it |
| **report** | two columns on the confirmed and probable tables, a paragraph that says what they mean, the fields in `report.json` | — | — |
| **review image** | one more caption line: `COLOUR: CLAY BODY DE76 … / SKIN HISTOGRAM DISTANCE … / NOT PART OF THE BAND` | — | — |

The colour columns, the caption line and the consensus rows exist **only where the collection has
colour to put on them** — the switch is the collection's own files, not a flag — which is what keeps
a colour-less collection's `report.md` and review images the bytes they were (§7). With `--objects
off` the object layer is absent entirely and with it every colour consensus row.

No test in the workspace asserts on `Evidence::colour`'s effect on a tier, because it has none. The
five tests added (444 → 449) assert what colour *is*, never what it decides:

* `the_colour_split_follows_the_labels` — a mesh whose shell and fracture faces carry different
  colours splits into two `ColourStats` that each see only their own, and a mesh with no colours
  splits into `(None, None)`.
* `the_lab_histogram_grid_is_fixed_and_clamped` — the 64 bins are the same 64 bins for every
  fragment, and a Lab value outside the grid lands in the edge bin instead of out of bounds.
* `the_colour_mad_is_a_median_and_not_a_mean` — one outlying vertex colour does not move the spread.
* `a_colour_consensus_is_never_measured_against_a_spread_it_does_not_have` — `mad_floor` is
  `COLOUR_MAD_FLOOR` for all nine Lab channels and **0.0** for every geometric key, which is what
  leaves `Consensus::mads` the test it was.
* `the_colour_evidence_is_absent_where_the_files_have_no_colour` — an SfS++ pair has no colour
  evidence at all, and a pair where only one half can be answered reports that half and `None` for
  the other.

## 10. What is open, for the rule S3 chooses

**Start here: 24 correct joins on `synthetic_mix3_24` fail on one thing, and it is not colour.**
Every accepted candidate carries `evidence.failed`, the list of confirmed-tier thresholds it missed.
Over seeds 0–4, of the 47 probable pairs on `synthetic_mix3_24`, **24 fail on nothing but the
support-or-margin arm** — `"neither arm: support 0 < 1 and no second placement to beat"` — and all 24
are true adjacent joins of one vessel. On `synthetic_pingsdorf_20` it is **37 of 77**, again all
true. These are the joins the workflow's own facts describe: R §5.7's keep returns one placement, so
there is no second placement to beat, and the pair has no independent neighbour to support it.

That population is where colour can raise recall, and the numbers say how far:

| set | probable pairs | failing **only** the arm | all correct? | `frac_delta_e` med / max | of them ≤ 5.0 | ≤ 7.5 |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| `synthetic_mix3_24` | 47 | **24** | yes, 24/24 | 2.43 / 11.13 | **18** | 20 |
| `synthetic_pingsdorf_20` | 77 | **37** | yes, 37/37 | 1.45 / 14.70 | **25** | 31 |

A third arm that reads `frac_delta_e ≤ 5.0` would offer **43 correct joins** across the two sets that
nothing else can reach — confirmed joins on `synthetic_mix3_24` would go from 39 to as many as 57
over five seeds.

**And it must not be written the naive way, for a reason this step measured by accident.** A veto and
a positive arm read a *constant* colour in opposite directions. `pot_H`'s eleven fragments carry one
flat grey (§2.3, reading 4), so on its 52 accepted candidates `frac_delta_e` is **5 × 10⁻¹²** and
`shell_hist` is **exactly 0** — the two sherds agree perfectly, because neither of them says anything.
A veto reads that as "no objection" and is safe; a positive arm reads it as *the strongest colour
evidence obtainable* and fires on every pair of every SfS++ collection — including all ten false
confirmed joins of `mixed_all`, whose 147 coloured fragments are that same grey. Only `pot_A` and
`pot_B`, which carry no colour array at all, are `None` and therefore genuinely abstain (measured:
71 of 71 accepted candidates on `pot_A`, 0 of 52 on `pot_H`).

So a positive colour arm needs a **third state** and not two: *disagrees*, *agrees*, and *has nothing
to say*. The distinguishing statistic is already in the cache and no rule reads it yet — a
`ColourStats` whose `lab_mad` is zero and whose histogram has one occupied bin is a fragment with no
colour information, whatever its mean says. S3 should require both sides to carry real variation
before an agreement counts as evidence. §5's other warning stands beside it: an arm this cheap will
also confirm the first cross-object pair R §6.5 accepts on a collection where two vessels' colours
agree by accident — `synthetic_170` is that collection (§2.3, reading 5).

**The one veto threshold this table supports, and its price.** `shell_hist ≥ 0.5` removes **99.0 %** of
`synthetic_mix3_24`'s different-object pairs at a cost of **0.0 %** of its adjacent ones, and on the
held-out twin `synthetic_mix3_60` **99.7 %** for **5.5 %**. On the accepted population it costs
literally nothing: the worst correct confirmed join on `mix3_24` sits at 0.41. That is the best row
in the whole sweep (`output/s2/measure/features.md` §"What an absolute threshold would remove").

**And the two controls that make it expensive.** On `synthetic_pingsdorf_20` — one vessel, so every
firing of the rule is a mistake — `shell_hist ≥ 0.5` refuses **17 of the 119 accepted joins** over
seeds 0–4, **5 of the 42 confirmed** ones, for zero gain. On the four real museum scans of the
terracotta, which are one object, the two accepted joins sit at **0.414 and 0.428** — under the limit,
but by less than the width of the `mix3_24` distribution, and on a sample of two. A veto tuned on
three vessels photographed separately is a veto tuned on three *photographs*; a real collection from
one kiln is `synthetic_170`, where every colour AUC is below chance (§2.3, reading 5).

**So the four questions S3 inherits:**

1. **Which statistic.** `shell_hist` has the separation, `frac_lab_a` has the physics (0.985/0.990 as
   a per-fragment channel, better than the pooled `frac_delta_e` at 0.935 — §2.3, reading 2). A rule
   on `|Δ frac_lab_a|` has not been swept and should be: the sweep in `features.md` covers
   `frac_delta_e`, `shell_delta_e`, `frac_hist` and `shell_hist` and not the single channel.
2. **Which direction.** Colour cannot remove a false join on any set this project has. The nine
   development sets produce **zero** false confirmed joins at every seed (§9), so there is nothing
   there to remove; the ten the workflow is chasing are on the acceptance set `mixed_all`, where
   every pair's colour distance is zero because every fragment is the same grey. Colour's only
   available contribution to the confirmation rule is therefore as **positive evidence** — which
   raises recall — not as a veto, which on these sets can only cost. That inverts the usual reading
   of §2's AUC, and the third-state warning above is the price of taking it.
3. **Which gate.** Any colour rule must be gated on the monochrome controls as well as the coloured
   sets, or it will pass on `mix3_24` and quietly refuse a seventh of `synthetic_pingsdorf_20`'s
   correct joins (§5).
4. **What a real collection would say.** Every number above is measured on synthetic sherds painted
   from five real photographs. The terracotta's four scans are the only real coloured fragments this
   project has and they are one object, so the *different*-object half of every colour measurement in
   this note is synthetic. A museum set of two vessels with photographic colour would be the single
   most valuable thing the project could acquire, and audit §D.2's leakage rule says it must not be
   the set the rule is fitted on.

**Two smaller things left open.** The 4×4×4 histogram's bin edges are fixed in Lab and a colour that
straddles one is read as two; a soft-assignment histogram would remove that and was not measured.
And `split_colour`'s nearest-centroid bridge assigns a file vertex the label of the closest working-
mesh face, which near a break's rim must put some skin vertices on a fracture face and the other way
round. **How much that contaminates the clay body was not measured** — the AUCs above are what the
bridge produces, not what a perfect split would — and it is the first thing to check if a rule on the
clay body ever behaves oddly on a thin wall, where the two sides are closest together.
