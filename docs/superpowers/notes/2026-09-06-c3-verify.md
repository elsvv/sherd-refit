Two things did not come out as the plan expected, and both are measurements rather than
concessions:

* **D §10.2's `pen` tolerance cannot be applied to a mesh with holes**, because Open3D's answer
  there is not a function of the geometry — its own `count_intersections` contradicts its own

* **D §10.2's native "accepted set identical" cannot hold at all.** The port scores what the
  reference scores exactly and then scores *different poses*, because the search above R §6 is not

## 1. What was ported

`crates/sherd-core/src/matching/verify.rs`, one function per R §6 subsection:

| R | function | what it measures |
|---|---|---|
| §6.1 | `fracture_scores` | `tight`, `gap`, `contact`, both ways round, from point-to-**surface** distances |
| §6.2 | `seam_score` | the length of A's breakline covered by B's, in `t/3` voxels |
| §6.3 | `continuity_scores` | the shell's step across the seam and the agreement of its normals |
| §6.4 | `penetration_scores` | the fraction of surface samples inside the other fragment, and the deepest |
| §6.5 | `accept` | the five limits at once |
| §5.4 | `Scores::partial` | the refusal a candidate cut before stage 2 carries |

`Scores` is D §4.1's struct and serialises to the reference's own JSON keys — `tightA`, `gapB`,
`cont_n`, the lot — with `partial` and `pen_unavailable` written as the numbers `1.0` the
reference writes and left out when unset. It reads the reference's `s2.scores.json` back, which is
how the parity row compares.

`Surfaces` is the view R §6 takes of one fragment: two BVHs, four point sets and a KD-tree over
the margin, built **once per pair** and read once per candidate. A stage-2 pair verifies ten poses
and neither the BVHs (19–41 ms each) nor the tree may be rebuilt for each of them. `Fragment`
gained the two `OnceLock`s D §4.1 specifies (`surface_scene`, `fracture_scene`), so a fragment in
nine pairs builds each BVH once.

`crates/sherd-core/src/matching/pair.rs` closes the chain: `SurfaceLadder` builds R §5.6's two
clouds and their trees once per pair, `Pair::stage2_candidate` runs the four rungs and R §6 on one
stage-1 pose (with R §5.6's early rejection, off by default), and `Pair::match_pair` drives
everything from R §5.1 to R §5.7 — including R §5.4's `stage1_floor` exit, also off by default.
The free `match_pair(a, b, p, keep)` applies R §4.1's wall-ratio test first and builds nothing for
a pair it refuses.

`crates/sherd-core/src/matching/screen.rs` is R §4.3: `cap`, `screen_pair` and `top_partners`,
off by default (`screen_top_k = 0`).

`crates/sherd-core/src/spatial/bvh.rs` gained the three queries R §6 measures through, all of them
`parry3d` calls E4 had already measured: `distance` (unbounded, ≤ 3.1e-5 against Embree on 300 000
queries), `bounded_distance` (D §6.5's `bounded_distance`, exact inside the window and 2.0–3.3×
faster than the unbounded call) and `inside` (three axis rays, a Möller–Trumbore count over the
leaves the ray's AABB test keeps, majority vote — E4's own implementation, which agreed with
Open3D on 270 000 of 270 000 queries on the nine watertight benchmark meshes).

**Three prunings that change no answer**, which is what PMC-11 and PMC-12 licence:

* the fracture distance runs bounded at `sc.facing`, because every use of it is a comparison
  against a threshold at or below `sc.facing` — `sc.tight`, `2·sc.tight`, `sc.facing` — so a point
  whose surface is further away needs no number, only the knowledge that it has none;
* the penetration test rejects on the mesh's AABB before it casts a parity ray, and computes a
  distance only for the points that are actually inside;
* when *nothing* is inside, R §6.4's `min(sd)` is the smallest positive distance, and a running
  minimum whose window is the best distance so far finds it without a single unbounded query.

## 2. Four details of the reference that a port gets wrong by default

**`tag "A"` is measured with `d2`.** `fracture_scores` computes `d1` from B's samples against A's
surface and `d2` the other way, and then pairs `("A", d2, A.frac_area)`: `tightA` is a statement
about *A's own points*. Writing the obvious pairing gives numbers that are right on a symmetric
pair and wrong on every other.

**`contact` is a mean over *all* points, not over the facing ones.** `tight` and `gap` are
conditioned on `d < sc.facing`; `contact` is `mean(d < 2·sc.tight)` over the whole sample set,
times the fracture area over `t²`. It is also computed only in the `else` branch — a fragment with
fewer than twenty facing points reports `contact = 0` rather than a small number.

**The seam is a length of A's curve in a grid fixed to the world origin.** `⌊P_A/(t/3)⌋` is
floored per axis on the *unmoved* A points, so the count depends on where A sits in space and not
only on the shape of the seam. Two implementations agree on it exactly or differ by whole voxels;
ours agrees exactly, on every candidate of every set.

**`frac_scene` falls back to `F[:1]`.** A fragment with no fracture face gets a scene over the
mesh's first triangle rather than an empty one, because a raycasting scene needs a triangle. R §6.1
now says so; the port does the same.

The one place the port deliberately differs is `np.linalg.inv(T)`: R §6.1 and R §6.4 invert the
pose with an LU factorisation of the full 4×4 and the port transposes the rotation. For a pose that
has been through thirty ICP iterations the two agree to about 1e-16, which moves a point a hundred
units from the origin by 1e-14 and no score by anything measurable — and the transpose needs no
factorisation to be reproducible on another machine.

matrices needs a tolerance. That comparison needs an *identity* test on the matrices, and
`pose_gap` is not one: its rotation goes through `acos`, which has a square-root singularity at
zero, so a pose that is orthonormal to 1e-16 is 8e-7 degrees from itself. Entry-by-entry equality
is what the row uses.

## 4. The `pen` row, and a mesh Open3D cannot answer for

R §3.3.2's `closed_enough` calls a working mesh watertight when under **0.2 % of its edges are
boundary edges** — deliberately, so that a decimated scan with a few holes still gets a
penetration test. On such a mesh "is this point inside" has no answer: a ray that leaves through a
hole crosses the surface one time fewer and the parity flips.

The first run of the `pen` row failed on 5 of pot A's 28 pairs, by up to 0.0027 against 0.0005,
and only on pairs involving `Pot_A_Piece_02_Mesh` (208 boundary edges) or `Pot_A_Piece_03_Mesh`
(149). The measurement that settles it, on `Pot_A_Piece_03_Mesh` (wall 3.45) against
`Pot_A_Piece_08_Mesh`'s 20 000 samples at the reference's own candidate 8:

| | Open3D | the port |
|---|---:|---:|
| samples called inside | 54 | 0 |
| depth of the deepest | 7.68 (**2.23 t**) | — |
| half the wall, i.e. the deepest a point of that solid can be | **1.72** | 1.72 |
| `compute_occupancy` agrees with `compute_signed_distance` | yes, on all 54 | — |
| `count_intersections` along `±x`, `±y`, `±z` | **even on all six, for all 54** | even |

Open3D's own ray counter contradicts its own occupancy on every one of those points, and the
geometry agrees with the port: no point of a 3.45-thick shell is 7.7 units inside it. The
reference is the side that is wrong there, and it is wrong because the question is ill-posed on a
mesh with 149 holes, not because Embree is.

The row therefore says what it can. `pen` is gated at D §10.2's 0.0005 on the **193 pairs whose
two working meshes are closed** (`n_boundary = 0`) — met, worst 5e-5, which is one sample of
20 000 — and reported as `pen (open mesh)` against `max_pen` on the **63 that have an open one**
(worst 2.7e-3, p99 2.1e-3, on pot A). On both kinds the *decision* is gated exactly and met
exactly: `pen limit` counts the candidates where the two sides disagree about `pen ≤ max_pen` and
`accepted` counts the ones where R §6.5's verdicts differ, and both are **zero on every pair of
every set**. R §6.4 and D §10.2 now carry this; PMC-7's row is where it belongs.

## 5. Natively the accepted sets differ, and the ground truth says the two are equally good

D §10.2's native `pair result` row asked for an identical accepted set and a best candidate within
1° / 0.05 t. Neither holds, and the reason is not in R §6:

* the port scores what the reference scores **exactly** (§3);
* natively it scores **different poses**. PMC-9 gives it different samples and therefore different
  breaklines and different fracture clouds; PMC-6 lets R §5.3's suppression keep a different set,
  which step C1 measured at 14.3 % of the reference's kept hypotheses on average and 43.6 % at
  worst. R §6.5 is a threshold on the output of that search, and R §13 already records that the
  decision "flips on nothing" for a candidate sitting on `min_tight`.

So the comparison that means something is against the **ground truth**, not against the reference.

collection's own `ground_truth.json` adjacency:

**On the terracotta the two accepted sets are identical and are exactly R §13's gate:** 021–094

pairs, which is a choice about memory and the GIL rather than a scaling limit inside the pair. Per-pair

and R §6 is the rest — ten candidates × (24 000 bounded fracture queries + 40 000 inside tests +
two KD-tree passes). The AABB reject is what keeps the penetration test off the critical path:
on a pair whose fragments are far apart at most poses, almost every one of the 400 000 inside
questions is answered by six comparisons.

## 7. Tests (+14; 245 → 259 passing, 1 ignored)

* **`verify.rs`, ten tests on hand-made geometry.** Two coincident walls with a 10 × 10 sample
  grid: `tight = 1`, `gap = 0`, `contact = area/t²`, and each of the three thresholds moved in
  turn — 0.005 t is still perfect contact, 0.05 t is no contact but a measured gap, 0.5 t is
  R §6.1's refusal. A second wall test puts A's samples on the surface and B's 0.05 t off it, so
  `tightA` and `tightB` land on opposite sides and `tight`/`gap` pick the worse one. A 30-point
  curve in a `t = 3` pair covers three voxels (`seam = 1`), covers none at 0.5 t away, covers none
  with the shell normals flipped, and covers six over twice the span. A 25-point margin grid gives
  `cont = 0`, `cont_n = 1`; raised by 0.05 t it gives `cont = 0.05`; turned over it gives
  `cont_n = −1`; with ten points it gives R §6.3's refusal. Two boxes give `pen = 0.5` and
  `pen_depth = 4`, and a third case shows why the vote is over *three* rays: a sample sitting
  exactly on a box face's triangulation diagonal is still found inside, which one ray would lose.
  R §6.5 is tested on each of its five limits at 1e-9 either side, and `Scores` round-trips
  through the reference's own JSON keys — including the reference's real `s2.scores.json` text.
* **`screen.rs`, three tests**: the cap is a sorted subset and a no-op when it fits, `top_partners`
  ranks inside each fragment's own row and keeps a pair either endpoint keeps, and a tie inside a

## 9. What is next

Phase 1d: R §8's assembly over the accepted candidates, R §9's full-resolution refinement, and
R §11's outputs — at which point R §13's benchmark gates can be run end to end, and the two
questions this step leaves open can be answered:

1. **Does the accepted-set difference of §5 reach the assembly?** The port accepts two joins on
   pot G that the reference refuses; both are true, and R §13 expects that set to produce no
   placement at all. The assembly's own consistency test is what decides, and it is the next step.
2. **Does `n_returned` matter?** A pair that returns three candidates instead of five hands the
   assembly a shorter list. On the sets measured the extra candidates are all refusals, so nothing
   changes; a pair whose *second* candidate is the true join is the case to watch.
