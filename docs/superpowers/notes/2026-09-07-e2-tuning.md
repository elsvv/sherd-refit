the memory model and the development-set decision"); working tree clean at the start, nothing left

## 3. E2.1 — D §5's shared `MatchData` cache

`matching::cache::MatchCache` is D §5's `Mutex<LruCache<(FragId, t_bits), Arc<MatchData>>>` of 64
entries, owned by the matching stage and read through `pair::match_pair_cached`. The lock is never
held across a build: a miss drops it, runs `MatchData::at`, and takes it again to insert, so two
threads racing on one key both build — the same bits twice, since the build is a pure function of
`(fragment, t, reg_points)` — and one handle is dropped.

The key carries the fragment's **address** beside its `FragId`, because `Fragment::from_mesh_file`
leaves `id` at zero and only the collection loader assigns it; a cache keyed on a field two live
fragments can share would serve one fragment's arrays for another.

E1 §5 predicted what it could remove and the counts came out exactly as predicted:

| set | lookups | hits | evictions | E1's prediction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| terracotta | 12 | 2 | 0 | the own-`t` half of 6 pairs |
| pot_H | 110 | **45** | 1 | "45 of 55 own-`t` builds" |
| synthetic 20 | 380 | **160** | 156 | "171 of 190", less what 64 entries evict |

| set | wall | CPU | peak RSS |
|---|---|---|---|
| terracotta | 3.76 → 3.89 s | 10.60 → 10.64 core-s | 481 → 494 MiB |
| pot_H | 11.56 → 11.05 s | 81.53 → 80.05 core-s (**−1.8 %**) | 526 → 530 MiB |
| synthetic 20 | 43.90 → 44.14 s | 317.90 → 314.96 core-s (**−0.9 %**) | 1 638 → 1 664 MiB |

So: **−0.9 % of CPU on the largest set, nothing on the wall clock, nothing on memory.** E1's
bracket was "at most 0.18 %", and the measured 0.9 % is above it for the reason E1 flagged when it
quoted the bracket — the ≤ 0.63 core-s it attributed to "clouds + the two KD-trees" counted only
the stacks that kept the `Pair::build` frame through a work-steal, so it was a lower bound on what
a cache could remove, not an upper one.

**The recommendation stands with the measurement, not against it.** The cache is 220 lines, it is
correct, it costs no measurable memory at 64 entries, and it removes about one per cent. It is
worth keeping because R §1.2's arithmetic will change the moment group-level matching (D §11,
roadmap item 5) makes a fragment's arrays serve more than one partner at one `t`; it is not worth
having built for its own sake, and E1 was right about that.

## 4. E2.2 — D §5 step 2's memory-aware semaphore

`sherd_core::memory` is the admission rule, the semaphore and the flag. A job reserves E1's
measured slope — `361 B` per original face, 344 MiB per million — and the `98 MiB` process floor
comes off the budget once rather than once per job, which is what E1 §7.1's four-concurrent
measurement settles. `--memory-budget GB` is D §9's flag on both `run` and `segment`, defaulting to
half of physical memory (`/proc/meminfo` on Linux, `sysctl hw.memsize` on macOS, 8 GiB assumed
where neither answers); `0` removes the bound.

The rule is four lines and one of them is the one that matters:

```rust
pub fn admits(budget: u64, in_flight: u64, running: usize, want: u64) -> bool {
    running == 0 || in_flight.saturating_add(want) <= budget
}
```

`running == 0` is what lets a scan larger than the whole budget run **alone** instead of waiting
for a reservation that can never be released. `memory::tests` checks the table D §8 prints (23, 11
and 2 concurrent scans of 1 M, 2 M and 10 M faces at an 8 GiB budget; 13, 6 and 1 at the 4.5 GiB
E1 §7.3 derives for a 170-fragment run), and two threaded tests check that eight jobs never
overlap more than the budget allows and that a budget of almost nothing serialises rather than
deadlocks.

The face count comes out of the PLY, OFF and binary-STL headers without reading the mesh; OBJ,
ASCII STL and GLB are estimated from the file size with per-format constants measured on this
project's own collections (the eleven pot_H OBJ files run 123.6–131.0 B a face; the constant is
100, so the estimate errs high and the semaphore errs towards admitting fewer jobs).

**Verification, terracotta, cold.** The default budget on this machine is 8 094 MiB and the four
scans reserve 1 491 MiB between them, so nothing waits. Forced to serialise:

| run | concurrent | waited | reservation held | wall | peak RSS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| default (8 094 MiB) | 4 | 0 | 1 491 MiB | 6.35 s | 1 451 MiB |
| `--memory-budget 0.5` | **1** | 3 | 461 MiB | 8.98 s | **835 MiB** |

The two runs write the same fourteen files — the four `cache/*.sherd` included — byte for byte,
and both are byte-identical to phase 1d's. The unbounded run's own reservation, 1 491 MiB, is
E1 §7.1's four-concurrent *measurement* (1 492 MiB) to a megabyte, which is the model checking
itself on a case it was not fitted to.

At the default budget nothing changed on the development sets: synthetic 20's warm CPU moved
314.96 → 316.14 core-s, inside the spread.

## 5. E2.3 — a bounding-box reject in front of every radius query

This is the commit that moved the numbers, and it is fifteen lines.

E1 measured that 70 % of the port's CPU is one `kiddo` bounded nearest-neighbour query, and named
R §5.2's coarse score as the caller: 60 probe points of B against A's whole breakline, for each of
tens of thousands of hypotheses. What that query mostly does is **say no**. A hypothesis of R §5.1
aligns *one* breakline frame of A with *one* of B, so most poses put most of the probe nowhere
near A's curve.

`examples/coarse_probe` classifies every one of those queries (it is committed, and it is how §7's
decision was made too):

| set | queries | **hit** | **outside A's breakline box, widened by the radius** | inside the box, no neighbour |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| synthetic 20, 4 pairs | 29.2 M | **2.85 %** | **74.97 %** | 22.18 % |
| pot_H, 8 pairs | 22.1 M | **14.35 %** | **65.25 %** | 20.40 % |

`PointTree` now carries the cloud's bounding box and tests it before the traversal. The argument is
one line: every point of the cloud is inside the box, so the distance from the query to the box is
a lower bound on the distance to any of them; if the box is further than the radius, the answer is
nothing. It holds whatever the tie rules are, because the filter never resolves a tie — it is a
filter in front of `kiddo`, not a second implementation of the query, and a query it lets through
is answered by exactly the code that answered it before. The only way it could be wrong is
arithmetically, and the comparison is widened by `16 ε` so that the true box distance clears the
radius even if every one of the five roundings in it went the other way.
`the_box_filter_never_rejects_a_neighbour_the_traversal_would_have_found` sweeps 400 queries
walking onto the box and 2 000 random ones at four radii each, including the exact distance and one
ULP either side of it.

It sits inside `nearest_within_squared` and `within_into`, which is **every** bounded query in the
port: R §5.2, R §5.4's re-score, R §6.2's seam, R §6.3's continuity, R §7's correspondence search
and R §3.4's ball queries.

| set | wall | CPU | matching |
|---|---|---|---|
| terracotta | 3.80 → **3.18 s** | 10.55 → **7.21** core-s (−32 %) | 1.14 → 0.70 s |
| pot_H | 10.74 → **8.97 s** | 80.23 → **62.55** core-s (−22 %) | 10.02 → 7.41 s |
| synthetic 20 | 44.26 → **27.61 s** (−38 %) | 316.14 → **182.27** core-s (−42 %) | 36.8 → 21.3 s |

Cold, with every one of the 35 caches rebuilt through R §3.4's and R §3.5's ball queries: the same
bytes.

## 6. E2.5 — the two centroid sweeps of R §3.5, bounded by the thresholds that read them

E1 §10's second item was R §4.2's rebuild: 9.6 % of synthetic 20 and all of it recomputation,
because `t_pair = min(t_A, t_B)` makes one of a pair's two fragments rebuild R §3.5 at the other's
wall thickness. A re-profile after E2.3 and E2.4 (§9) says where inside it, on synthetic 20's
169.5 core-s:

| inside R §4.2's rebuild | core-s | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| `breakline::build_with` — the points, their tree, and **the distance from every face centroid to the nearest of them** | **22.72** | 13.4 % |
| `face_adjacency` | 2.96 | 1.7 % |
| `breakline_distance` — the same question for the 20 000 surface samples | 2.12 | 1.2 % |
| `sample_on_faces` | 0.52 | 0.3 % |
| the macro normals themselves (the `0.15 t – 0.60 t` annulus) | 0.42 | 0.2 % |
| `face_geometry` | 0.41 | 0.2 % |
| `PointTree::build` | 0.29 | 0.2 % |

Almost all of it is one line, and that line is an **unbounded** nearest-neighbour search run once
per face of a 200 000-face working mesh (`nearest_distance` alone is 18.8 core-s of self time,
11 % of the whole profile, at ~495 ns a query). E1 proposed caching it per fragment, which would
work and would cost about 20 MB a fragment. It is not necessary, because of what the array is
*for*:

* `macro_normals` reads it only as `distance[i] >= inner`, `inner = 0.15 t`;
* `margin_indices` reads `d_brk` only as `inner < d < outer`, `outer = 1.5 t`.

Neither ever needs the exact value of a distance beyond its own threshold. `nearest_below(c, r)`
answers `Some(d)` exactly when `d < r` and `None` otherwise, so mapping `None` to `∞` reproduces
both predicates exactly — `∞ >= inner` is the same `true`, `∞ < outer` the same `false` — and the
bounded search now also gets E2.3's box in front of it, which is decisive here: a working mesh has
200 000 centroids and a breakline of a few thousand points, so nearly every centroid is far away.
`the_bounded_breakline_distance_is_the_exact_one_under_its_bound` checks the equivalence on 2 000
queries at five bounds against the exact sweep.

`breakline_distance` itself is left exact and public, because the parity harness compares that
array against the reference's; the bounded form is a second function used by `samples::build`.

| set | wall | CPU |
|---|---|---|
| terracotta | 2.41 → 2.41 s | 7.02 → **6.76** core-s (−3.7 %) |
| pot_H | 8.43 → 8.71 s | 61.32 → **60.95** core-s |
| synthetic 20 | 23.67 → **22.95 s** | 172.85 → **158.21** core-s (**−8.5 %**) |

Cold, all 35 caches rebuilt: the same bytes. This is the one commit whose wall-clock share (3 %)
is under the task's 5 % stopping rule while its CPU share (8.5 %) is well over it — the work it
removes was spread evenly over ten threads. CPU is the number the 170-fragment projection is built
on, so it counts.

## 7. E2.6 — D §6.2's grid, as a mask rather than as a search

After E2.3 the coarse score was still the largest single item, 63.1 core-s of 169.5. The box
answers three quarters of its queries; what is left is the quarter that lands *inside* A's
breakline box and still finds nothing — 22 % of all queries against 2.9 % that hit. A breakline is
a **curve**, so its bounding box is the fragment's box and almost all of that volume is empty.

D §6.2's uniform grid is the structure that knows this, and E3 measured it at 0.4–2.0× `kiddo` as a
*search*, which is why the port never built one. As a **mask** it is a different proposition:
`spatial::grid::NearMask::may_be_near` returns `false` only when nothing is within the radius, and
`true` means "ask the tree". It resolves nothing, so it cannot resolve a tie differently.

The layout is chosen so that a query is one lookup:

* cell side `max(longest extent / 63, radius) · (1 + 1e-9)`, so the grid is at most 64³ cells and a
  cell is never narrower than the radius;
* `z` packed into one `u64` per `(x, y)` column — the whole mask is ≤ 32 KiB, L1-resident while a
  pair's hypotheses are scored;
* **dilated once at build time** by three word-wide OR passes, so each cell records whether its
  3×3×3 block holds a point. Because a cell is at least as wide as the radius, a neighbour within
  the radius is in the query's own cell or one of the 26 around it, and the dilated bit covers all
  27. A query outside the grid is clamped to the boundary cell, whose block is a superset of the
  block the true index names, so clamping only ever makes the answer more permissive.

The `1 + 1e-9` on the cell side is the whole of the floating-point care: it makes `radius / cell`
strictly below one by far more than the rounding of `(x − lo)·(1/cell)` can move a floor, so the
index of a point and the index of a query a radius apart never differ by more than one cell.
`the_mask_never_hides_a_neighbour` checks the promise against brute force on 60 000 queries over
three cloud shapes — random, a curve, a plane — at five radii from a twentieth of a cell to larger
than the cloud.

One mask is built per pair over A's breakline at `sc.coarse`, before the hypothesis `par_iter`;
`agreeing` consults it and falls through to the same `nearest_below` on `true`.

| set | wall | CPU | matching |
|---|---|---|---|
| terracotta | 2.41 → 2.46 s | 6.76 → 6.86 core-s | 0.66 → 0.63 s |
| pot_H | 8.71 → 8.76 s | 60.95 → 61.47 core-s | 7.38 → 7.32 s |
| synthetic 20 | 22.95 → **19.50 s** | 158.21 → **130.4** core-s (−18 %) | 18.60 → **14.86 s** |

The two small sets do not move because their coarse stage was never the cost: pot_H's fragments
are 7.6 k–42 k-face sherds whose hypotheses are 4.9× fewer per pair than synthetic 20's, which is
E1 §4.3's finding and the reason the mask is worth what it is worth on the set that decides the
gates and nothing on the sets that do not.

The grid is **not** used as a search anywhere, and `examples/coarse_probe` is the measurement that
says why it should not be: at the cell counts a curve through a 64³ grid actually needs, answering
the query from the grid means 27 to 125 cell probes and a tie rule of its own, against a `kiddo`
descent E1 measured at 99 ns. One bit and a fall-through is the whole of what pays.

## 8. E2.7 — R §11.5's views in parallel

With R §11.4's meshes parallel, the previews were the largest wall-clock item outside the matcher:
3.37 s of synthetic 20's 19.50, on 2.25 core-seconds of work. `render_views` drew one 900×700 view
at a time; a view is one image over its own z-buffer and the strip is assembled by index, so the
views are `rayon`'s and no pixel depends on which thread drew one.

What is deliberately **not** touched is the sampling above them. R §11.5 draws every fragment's
preview points from one `rng(0)` walked in group order and then in collection order (R §10);
reordering that would change the points and with them the PNGs. That is also why this is the last
commit of the step: what is left of the preview stage is that sequential draw and the
`face_geometry` it needs, and hoisting the geometry into a parallel pre-pass is worth about 0.5 s
of synthetic 20's 17.8 — under the 5 % of wall clock the task set as the stopping rule — at the
cost of holding twenty fragments' geometry (≈ 280 MB) at once.

Measured by **interleaving** the two binaries, three runs each, because the machine had drifted:

| binary | `output` stage | wall |
|---|---:|---:|
| `114455a` | 3.32 s | 22.45 s |
| `2df23e8` | **2.17 s** (−35 %) | **20.26 s** (−9.8 %) |

CPU is unchanged at 135–137 core-s, which is the point: this moves work onto idle cores rather
than removing it.

`nearest_within_squared`, and E2.3's box sits in front of it too. That is the shape of this whole
step — one filter, six call sites.

Two symbols are still 51 % of the profile (`kiddo`'s bounded search and the wrapper around it),
down from E1's 70 %, and `coarse::agreeing_masked` is now 13 % on its own: the loop that transforms
sixty points and asks the mask is itself a measurable part of the coarse stage, which is what
happens when the query it guards gets cheap.

### 10.1 Per pair, one thread, warm

The port's own `pair matched … seconds=` line, `--threads 1 --no-preview --no-meshes`.

against phase 1d's 1.039, 1.190 and 1.332: **2.0×, 1.4× and 2.6×** cheaper per pair.

D §1's two pair rows, each answered by the closest thing actually run:

| D §1 target | Python today | target | phase 1d | E2 | verdict |

The shape is E1 §8's, unchanged by anything in this step: flat CPU to six threads on the

### 10.3 The projection for the large sets — still a projection

Same arithmetic as E1 §6.2, on the new per-pair cost and the measured 6.43× pool:

| set | pairs | per pair (basis) | core-s | ÷ 6.43 | E1's projection | gate |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| synthetic 60 | ≈ 1 770 | 0.523 (synthetic 20) | 926 | **2.4 min** | 6.1 min | ≤ 15 min |
| synthetic 170 | ≈ 12 800 | 0.523 (synthetic 20) | 6 694 | **17.4 min** | 44 min | ≤ 2 h |
| `mixed_all` | 12 589 | 0.870 (pot_H) | 10 952 | **28.4 min** | 39 min | ≤ 2 h |

Preprocessing adds little: 170 scans of the synthetic kind at ~3 core-seconds each is 8.5
core-minutes, about 1.5 minutes of wall clock at the concurrency the semaphore of §4 admits.

The two caveats E1 stated are unchanged and are restated here because a better projection is not a
run. The synthetic figures are **upper bounds** in the one way that matters — the 60- and
170-piece sets cut the *same* pot the 20-piece set cuts, so their fragments are smaller, and §9.1
shows the coarse score is still the term that grows with fragment size. And the projection cannot
see anything that is not linear in the pair count: R §8's assembly over a few thousand accepted
candidates, R §9's refinement over 170 placed fragments, the merged writer. On synthetic 20 those
are 0.03 s and 1.14 s against 14.64 s of matching, so they would have to grow by two orders of
magnitude to matter — which only the final acceptance run settles.

The semaphore of §4 is what makes the memory side of that run possible, and E1 §7.3's arithmetic is
unchanged by this step: for 170 scans of 1–2 M faces on a 16 GB machine the budget has to be about
4.5 GiB, and then the pool is held to 13 concurrent scans at 1 M faces and **6** at 2 M.

## 11. What E2 leaves

**Stopping rule.** The task's rule was to stop when the next item is worth under 5 % of synthetic
20's wall clock. It is:

| what is left | core-s | wall | why it is not done |
|---|---:|---|---|
| R §5.6's four ICP rungs | 35.9 (27 %) | ~5 s | E1 §10.5: already 5–10× the reference (C2 §7), and every change to it is a change to a *result* |
| R §5.2's coarse score, what the mask lets through | 36.0 (27 %) | ~5 s | 2.9 % of its queries hit; the rest is the tree's own floor. A tighter filter than a box plus a dilated cell is a k-DOP or a two-level box, and neither was measured |
| R §6's verification | 13.2 (10 %) | ~2 s | `parry3d` point-to-triangle and the ray-parity inside test; both are R §6 verbatim |
| R §4.2's remaining pair setup | 16.0 (12 %) | ~2.5 s | the clouds, the two KD-trees and the `f64` widenings; E1's per-fragment cache would take part of it at ~20 MB a fragment |
| R §11.5's preview sampling | 2.4 (2 %) | ~0.5 s | **under the rule** — one seeded stream in a fixed order (R §10), so only the `face_geometry` under it can be hoisted, at 280 MB |
| allocator | ~7 (5 %) | — | spread over the ICP ladder and verification; no single site |

**Open issues E2 leaves.**

1. The `MatchData` cache (§3) earns about one per cent today and is kept for the reason §3 gives,
   not for that per cent. If group-level matching (D §11, roadmap item 5) lands, its arithmetic
   changes and the number should be re-measured, not assumed.
2. `spatial::grid::NearMask` is a second use of D §6.2's cell layout on the CPU while the GPU
   kernel of phase 2b still expects the *search* form. The two share a design and no code, and
   phase 2b should decide whether the WGSL side wants the mask as well — the measurement that says
   the CPU search does not pay is in `examples/coarse_probe`, and it is the CPU's measurement.
3. Peak RSS rose 19 % on synthetic 20 (§9). It is inside its own run-to-run spread and 3.2× inside
   D §8's gate, but it is the one number that moved the wrong way and the 170-fragment run is the
   one that will say whether it matters.
4. The projection of §10.3 is 2.5× better than E1's and still a projection. It does not discharge
   D §10.3's three large rows.
5. `dsymutil target/release/sherd-refit-rs` leaves a 32 MB `.dSYM` under `target/` (gitignored);
   it is needed to re-run §9.1's profile and was left in place.
