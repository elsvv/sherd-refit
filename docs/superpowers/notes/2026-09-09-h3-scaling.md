# H3 — the scaling fixes and `--seed`: which stage holds the memory, and what a seed moves

**Date:** 2026-09-09. **Tree:** branch `rust-core`, `11d658b` (H2) → `H3.1…H3.6`. **Machine:**
Apple M2 Pro, 10 cores (6P + 4E), 16-core GPU, 16 GB, macOS 24.6.0, rustc 1.97.0, `--release`.
Nothing above 20 fragments was matched. Task H3 executes step 4 of the audit's plan §E
(`notes/2026-09-09-fable-audit.md`): §B.2, §B.3, and §C.3's missing `--seed`.

**The three sentences.** R §9's refinement read one original scan per grouped fragment with no
reservation at all, in a `par_iter` as wide as the machine; it now holds the same `MemorySemaphore`
permit preprocessing and R §11.4's writers hold, and under a budget that admits nothing it reads
one scan at a time and produces the same clouds bit for bit. Both BVHs lived for the process
although their last readers end with matching — measured at **129 B and 12.5 B per working-mesh
face**, which is 472 MB and 46 MB on `synthetic_20` and about **4.4 GB and 0.4 GB** at D §8's 170
fragments — and they are released after the second pass now, before the two stages that hold a
full-resolution original per job. And `--seed N` exists on `run` and `bench`, so R §13's spread can
be asked for on the port's side instead of constructed by hand on the reference's: the terracotta
keeps its two joins at all five seeds, and the number of *accepted* candidates behind them does not
(6, 7, 7, 8, 9).

## 1. §B.2 — R §9's refinement under the semaphore

The audit's §B.2 is one sentence of code and one of reasoning. Preprocessing reserves E1's measured
`361 B` a face before it opens a scan (`pipeline.rs`, D §5 step 2) and so do R §11.4's placed
writers (`report.rs`, E2); R §9's cloud loop opened one original per grouped fragment and reserved
nothing, so the stage's high-water mark was `pool width × original` — nine multi-million-face scans
at once on this machine, however large they are.

The fix is the same three lines the other two stages use, held across `load_mesh` and
`fracture_cloud` and dropped the moment the cloud is the only thing left. It moves no result, for
the reason no admission rule does: the loop collects by index, each job reads only its own file, and
R §9's cap is drawn from `refine::CAP_SEED` — the literal 0 the reference hard-codes at
`refine.py:35` — and not from the schedule.

**Test** (`pipeline::tests::the_refinement_reserves_its_scans_like_preprocessing`): the two slab
pieces, once under `Budget::unbounded()` and once under `Budget::bytes(1)`. The bounded run reports
`peak_running == 1` — one scan at a time — and every cloud's `idx`, points and normals are compared
**as bits** against the unbounded run's, not with `==`.

The stage now logs what the semaphore did, exactly as preprocessing does, whenever the budget is
bounded.

## 2. §B.3 — the BVHs, measured before they were decided on

### 2.1 What they cost

D §8 booked both trees "in the LRU" and there has never been an LRU: they are `OnceLock`s on
`Fragment` and they live for the process. The row also guessed 4 MB for the full tree. Measured on
`synthetic_20` (20 fragments, **3 658 512** working-mesh faces) by building every tree between two
readings of the resident set:

| | total | per fragment | per working-mesh face | at D §8's 170 × 200 k faces |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| whole-mesh BVH (R §6.4's penetration) | **472 MB** | 23.6 MB | **129 B** | **4.4 GB** |
| fracture BVH (R §6.1's point-to-surface) | **46 MB** | 2.3 MB | **12.5 B** | **0.4 GB** |

So the audit's estimate of the problem (2–3 GB at 170 fragments) was low, and it was the *wrong
tree* that dominates: the fracture tree, the one §B.3 names, is a tenth of the whole-mesh one.

### 2.2 Drop, not an LRU — and why

Both readers stop before the run does: R §6.1's fracture tree with the last pair, R §6.4's
whole-mesh tree with the last `try_place`, and R §8.2's recentring reads only the surface samples.
So there is a **release point**, after the second pass and before R §9 and R §11 — the two stages
that hold one full-resolution original per job. An LRU has nothing to offer against that: during
all-pairs matching every fragment's tree is live at once, so a cache smaller than the collection
only thrashes and one as large as it is exactly what the `OnceLock`s already were.

One structural change was needed. R §8's `Piece` list borrowed the trees from the fragments and
therefore kept the collection immutably borrowed to the end of the run; it now holds the whole-mesh
tree's `Arc` (`Fragment::surface_scene_arc`), and R §8.2's own view of the pieces carries no tree at
all. The release is a reset and not a poisoning: a fragment asked for a tree afterwards builds it
again, which the test asserts by putting the same closest-face query through both trees on both
sides of `release_scenes`.

### 2.3 The measurement, per stage

The instrument is new and ships: `memory::RssMonitor` samples the process's resident set on a thread
of its own every 100 ms (`/proc/self/statm` on Linux, `ps` on macOS — the workspace denies
`unsafe_code`; the `ps` costs a measured 4.2 ms), `pipeline::StageLog` closes a window wherever the
run writes a timing, and the number goes to the stage's log line, to the table `run` and `bench`
print, and to `report.json`'s new `memory` block beside `timings`.

`run input/synthetic_pingsdorf_20/fragments --backend cpu`, warm cache, previews and meshes **on**,
three runs each, medians (spread in brackets), MiB:

| stage | trees held (before) | trees released (after) |
|---|---|---|
| preprocess | 311 (309–340) | 322 (300–343) |
| matching | 1 606 (1 580–1 607) | 1 664 (1 586–1 680) |
| assembly | 1 576 (1 572–1 607) | 1 586 (1 491–1 604) |
| **refine** | **1 627** (1 618–1 739) | **1 459** (1 394–1 532) |
| output | 1 610 (1 593–1 739) | 1 605 (1 281–1 784) |
| **returned at the release point** | **0** | **205–275** |

Read honestly, this table says three things and not four:

* the release returns **205–275 MiB** to the operating system at the moment it happens, in every
  run, deterministically — that column is a direct before/after reading and not a difference of
  medians;
* the **refinement** stage is **168 MiB** lower at the median, about 10 %, and its whole band
  (1 394–1 532) sits below the held band (1 618–1 739), so the effect is larger than the spread on
  that stage;
* the **output** stage does not move: it is dominated by R §11.4's parallel writers, each holding
  one original under D §5's budget, and its own spread (±20 %) swallows a quarter of a gigabyte.
  The **whole-run** peak on this set does not move either, because it is *matching's*, which is
  before the release point.

`synthetic_20` is 20 fragments of 183 k faces. The reason to make the change is the last column of
§2.1's table, not this one: on the museum's 170 the same constants put 4.4 GB of trees under R §9
and R §11 on a 16 GB machine, and the release is free — no rebuild, no policy, no key.

### 2.4 A note on what "reclaimed" means

The trees are 518 MB and the release returns 205–275 MiB of resident set. The rest is not leaked: it
is in the allocator's free lists, where the next stage's allocations reuse it. That is why the
stage-level numbers move less than the tree sizes, and it is the honest limit of measuring a heap
through RSS.

## 3. §C.3 — `--seed N`

R §13's per-set rows are a **spread** the reference produces under `Params(seed = 0…4)`, and neither
CLI could ask for it — the port's byte-determinism per seed then reads as stability, which the audit
§C.9 says it is not. `--seed` sets `Params.seed`, which R §10 already carried to R §5.2's coarse
probe and R §4.3's screening cap; the third consumer, R §3.5's three per-fragment samplers, built at
a **hard-coded 0**. That was the whole of the gap.

`SampleParams::at` takes the seed now, so every call site says which one it means, and
`Fragment::from_mesh_file`, `load_or_build`, `rebuild_samples` and `preprocess` carry it from the
run. The cache needed nothing new: `md_params` has always held the seed, so a cache written at
another seed has R §3.5's three arrays recomputed **and nothing else** — the mesh, the labels and
the breaklines are functions of the file — which is R §3.7's own rule, asserted in
`the_seed_moves_the_sampled_arrays_and_nothing_else`. R §9's cap and R §11.5's previews keep the
literal 0 the reference hard-codes for them (V4-D9): the reference's refinement does not answer to
its own seed and neither does the port's. `segment` takes no `--seed`, and a test asserts that
rather than leaving it to be noticed. The seed is recorded in `engine` in **both** output files.

### 3.1 Seeds 0–4 on the terracotta

`run --seed k --no-meshes --no-preview --backend cpu`, R §13's row is the joins:

| seed | joins used | accepted candidates | groups | tight | seam (t) | pen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 021–094, 094–104 | 7 | 3 + 1 alone | 0.557 / 0.535 | 20.67 / 12.33 | 0 / 0 |
| 1 | 021–094, 094–104 | 8 | 3 + 1 alone | 0.550 / 0.568 | 20.67 / 12.33 | 0 / 0 |
| 2 | 021–094, 094–104 | 7 | 3 + 1 alone | 0.542 / 0.553 | 20.67 / 12.00 | 0 / 0 |
| 3 | 021–094, 094–104 | 6 | 3 + 1 alone | 0.564 / 0.557 | 21.00 / 11.33 | 0 / 0 |
| 4 | 021–094, 094–104 | 9 | 3 + 1 alone | 0.543 / 0.521 | 21.00 / 12.33 | 0 / 0 |

**R §13's terracotta row holds at every seed**: exactly the two joins, 007 unplaced, both `pen` 0,
both `tight` far above 0.27, and the two seams inside 20 % of 20.3 t and 10.7 t (the bands are
16.3–24.4 and 8.5–12.8; the measurements are 20.67–21.00 and 11.33–12.33).

What does move is the number of *accepted* candidates behind those two joins — **6 to 9** — and the
scores themselves in the third digit. That is the seed spread the reference's own table is made of,
made visible on the port for the first time, and it is exactly the material roadmap item 3's
stability tier (audit §D.1) is meant to consume.

### 4.1 The exempt files, named

The byte-identity comparison covers `transforms.json`, `report.json`, `report.md`, every

a difference this task's brief names:

* **`report.json`'s `memory`** — new: the sampled per-stage peak resident set. It is a measurement

* **`engine.commit`** — changes with every commit, as it has at every step.

## 6. What this task did not do

* **No LRU.** §2.2 argues against it on the measurement rather than deferring it; if group-level
  matching (roadmap item 5) ever makes the live set smaller than the collection, the argument
  changes and the release point stays useful anyway.
* **The `output` stage's own peak is untouched.** It is R §11.4's writers holding originals under
  D §5's budget, which is already the semaphore's business; the way to lower it is a smaller
  `--memory-budget`, not another release.
* **`--seed` is not on `segment`.** A cache written at one seed has R §3.5's three arrays recomputed
  by the run that wants another; giving `segment` a seed would only let an operator warm a cache the
  next run will partly discard.
* **Nothing was measured above 20 fragments**, so §2.1's last column is an extrapolation from a
  per-face constant and says so. Step 12 of the audit's plan is where it becomes a measurement.
