## 1. What was ported

Three area-weighted samples and the runtime structure built on them.

1. **`S`, `sp` (R §3.5.1).** `surface_points = 20 000` points over the whole surface, with the face
   each came from. `sample_on_faces`: `p = A/ΣA` over the selected faces (numpy's pairwise sum),
   a sequential `cumsum` normalised by its last entry, `n` uniforms through
   `searchsorted(..., 'right')` for the face, then `n` for `u` and `n` for `v` with the
   `u + v > 1 → (1−u, 1−v)` fold, then `V[F₀] + u(V[F₁]−V[F₀]) + v(V[F₂]−V[F₀])`.
2. **`Pf`, `fp` (R §3.5.2).** `n_frac = int(clip(150·fracture_area/t², 5000, 12000))` points over
   the fracture faces alone, drawn the same way. The count is a *density*, which is what describes
   a big sherd and a small one equally finely; the clamps keep the ICP affordable at the top and
   meaningful at the bottom.
3. **`margin_idx` (R §3.5.6).** `d_brk` from every surface sample to the nearest breakline point
   (the *whole* breakline, not the hypothesis subset), then
   `¬frac[sp] ∧ 0.12 t < d_brk < 1.5 t`, thinned to 6 000 by a sorted draw without replacement —
   and only when it does not already fit, which is why a small margin consumes no random numbers
   at all. Both bounds are strict and neither is resolution-floored (PMC-5).
4. **`MatchData` (R §3.6),** built per `(fragment, t)` and never stored: `SN = FN[sp]`,
   `Nf = FN[fp]`, `S_frac = frac[sp]`, `Pm`/`Nm`, the breakline's tangent and dihedral, the four
   point clouds `pc_reg` / `pc_frac` / `pc_brk` / `pc_brk_full`, and the two KD-trees. `pc_reg`'s
   split is R §3.6's: `nf' = max(1, round(nf·6000/(nf+nm)))` with numpy's round-half-to-even, and
   prefixes of both arrays, because a prefix of an i.i.d. area-weighted draw is still an
   area-weighted sample. `tree_frac` is not built (PMC-14).

`MatchData::at(fragment, t, reg_points)` is R §4.2: at the fragment's own `t` it borrows the
cached arrays, at any other `t` it rebuilds **both** halves — breaklines first, because the samples
measure `d_brk` against them — and the rebuild is identical to a from-scratch build.

## 2. The RNG, and the one deliberate departure from the reference's structure

The reference builds **one** `default_rng(seed)` per fragment and runs all of R §3.5 through it, so
the fracture samples depend on how many numbers the surface samples consumed. This port keeps the
draw order *inside* each sampler — `n` for the face pick, `n` for `u`, `n` for `v` — and gives each
sampler **its own stream** (`rng::Draw`, D §7). Two reasons, and neither is taste:

* R §4.2 and R §8 rebuild the arrays at another `t` and, in the assembly's case, at another
  `surface_points`. With one shared stream, changing the *first* sampler's count silently redraws
  the other two; with one stream per draw site, a sampler that did not change does not move.
* The separation cannot be "the same seed three times". That would hand the surface sampler and
  the fracture sampler the identical stream of uniforms, and a surface sample and a fracture sample
  landing on the same face would then land on *the same point of it*. Each `Draw` therefore carries
  a fixed 64-bit tag (the FNV-1a hash of its name) exclusive-or'd into the seed, and
  `ChaCha8Rng::seed_from_u64` runs its own SplitMix64 over the result.

`Draw::Thickness` is tagged **zero**, so R §3.2's stream is exactly the one steps B1 and B2
measured every native number against; moving it would have moved every `t` in the tree for nothing.
The uniform itself is numpy's own construction, `(word >> 11)·2⁻⁵³` — the same 53-bit resolution
and the same half-open range over ChaCha8's words instead of PCG64's, so the port draws *the same
estimator on a different sample*. R §12's PMC-9 row now says all of this; it previously said only
"portable RNG, same draw structure".

`thickness::sample_face_indices` moved into `rng::without_replacement` so that R §3.2's draw and
R §3.5.6's thinning are one partial Fisher–Yates rather than two.

## 3. Where it lives

`Fragment.samples: Samples` is built at the fragment's own `t` right after the breaklines, from the
same `f64` geometry the segmentation ran on. `Fragment` also gained `area` and `frac_area` —
R §3.4's two sums, which it was recomputing the whole face geometry for on every call — and both
travel in the cache metadata, so `fracture_fraction()` is now a division rather than a pass over
200 000 faces.

The cache gains `S`, `Pf` (`f32[n,3]`) and `sp`, `fp`, `margin_idx` (`u32[n]`), and the metadata
gains `md_params` (`t`, the seed and the four counts). `CACHE_VERSION` 3 → 4. Three rules the
reader enforces that the writer cannot: `sp`/`fp` must describe `S`/`Pf`, every sample face must be
a face of `F`, and every `margin_idx` must index `S`. R §3.7's rule is now implemented for both
halves: a cache whose `md_params` are not the run's has *those five arrays* redrawn and rewritten,
with the mesh, the labels and the breaklines still coming off the disk.

**The narrowing happens once, and everything derived follows it.** `S` and `Pf` are `f32` in the
cache, so they are narrowed as soon as they are drawn and `d_brk`, the margin band and `Pm` are
computed from the *narrowed* values — the rule `WorkingMesh::from_parts` obeys for the mesh, for
the same reason: every stored array is a function of the other stored arrays and of nothing wider.

`sherd-refit-rs segment` prints `frac pts` and `margin` columns and logs the three counts and the
sampling time per fragment.

Four of these are worth reading twice.

* **`n_frac` is exact on all 66,** and 32 of them have a count strictly between the two clamps, so
  the rule is genuinely exercised rather than saturated. It is the one place the fracture area
  enters the sampling, and reproducing it exactly means the port's `A[frac].sum()` — numpy's
  pairwise summation over the selected faces — and its `int(clip(...))` are the reference's.
* **`margin count` and `margin members` are exact on all 66.** The port recomputes R §3.5.6 from
  the reference's own `S`, `sp` and `brk_P`: a KD-tree query per sample, then the band. The size of
  the resulting mask equals the reference's own `n_margin` (from `md.rng.json`, the count *before*
  thinning) entry for entry, and every index the reference kept is in it. Equal counts plus
  containment is equality, so the two masks are the same mask — which is as exact as a comparison
  of a derived array can be when the array it is derived from was drawn by another generator.
* **`S on face` is the reference's own round-off, not the port's.** It measures the *reference's*
  points against the triangles the *reference's* own `sp` names, using this port's geometry:
  3.3e-11 at coordinates of a few hundred is `f64` round-off in `V[F₀] + u·e₁ + v·e₂`. What it
  actually tests is the port's reading of `sample_on_faces`'s index convention — a port that built
  its points from the wrong two edges would report a residual here instead of an opinion.
* **`fracture faces` is exact:** every one of the reference's fracture samples sits on a face the
  dump labels fracture, on every fragment.

### 4.1 The normal check needed a conditioning rule, and the rule is arithmetic

The two normal checks first read 0.155–0.397° on three of the twenty synthetic fragments, against a
0.1° gate. That is not the port's arithmetic; it is the reference's own mesh.

Narrowing a vertex to `f32` moves it by up to one ulp of its coordinate, and moving a vertex by `δ`
perpendicular to the opposite edge turns the face normal by `δ/h`, where `h` is that vertex's
altitude. The reference's decimated working meshes carry slivers. On `frag_008`:

* the worst face of the whole mesh has edges 0.537, 0.537 and **7.7e-8** — an aspect ratio of
  7 × 10⁶ — and the narrowing turns its normal by a full **90°**;
* the worst face any *sample* landed on has edges 0.638, **0.0023**, 0.640 at coordinates of 463,
  whose short edge is 77 `f32` ulps, and the narrowing turns its normal by 0.397°;
* 525 of its 126 236 faces (0.42 %) move more than 0.1°, and together they are **1.2e-5 of the
  area** — which is exactly why an area-weighted sample of 20 000 points hits one about a quarter
  of the time, and why three of twenty fragments show it and none of the pots do.

Measured against the model bound `ulp/h`, the observed angle is at most **1.39×** on every one of
the 66 fragments (median well under 1.1×), so the bound is the whole story. The check therefore
takes its worst case over the faces with `h ≥ 1000 ulp` — which bounds the turn at 1e-3 rad =
0.057°, under the 0.1° gate **by construction** rather than by measurement — and gates the share of
samples left out at 0.1 %, measured maximum 0.033 %. Filtered, the worst normal over all 66
fragments is 0.038°.

This is a note about the *input*, not about the port: a handful of samples out of 20 000 carrying
an ill-defined normal contributes 1/20 000 of the weight of a point-to-plane ICP system, and the
area they represent is 1e-5 of the surface. It is recorded because a later stage reading a normal
at a sample should know what the worst case is and why.

(cells are "failures, worst ratio of the tolerance"; 1.00 is the gate.)

The `spacing` rows need one sentence of definition. Two independent area-weighted samples of the
same surface are two different point sets, so what can be compared is their *density*: for a
Poisson process of `n` points over an area `A`, the 95th percentile of the nearest-neighbour
distance is `√(ln 20 / π)·√(A/n) = 0.977·√(A/n)`, and two i.i.d. samples of the same surface give
that same figure whether it is measured within one set or between the two. The check measures the
cross-set p95 (the worse direction) against that expectation and allows a factor of two. No
per-fragment scale has to be invented, and the statistic is a property of the surfaces rather than
of the sampler.

**And it says the sampler is right.** The measured cross-set p95 of the 20 000 surface samples
agrees with the prediction to within **1.34 % on every one of the 66 fragments**, median 0.48 %.
That is the strongest available statement about a sample PMC-9 forbids comparing directly: the
port's draw is an independent sample of the same area-weighted distribution, at the same density,
on the same surface.

### 5.1 The six failures, measured

All four fragments are named by rows above this one. Their working meshes were read out of the
port's own caches (`sherd-refit-rs segment` into a scratch directory, the `.sherd` tensors read
straight out of the safetensors file) against the dump's `mesh.*`, `seg.frac_final` and `md.*`, so
both sides are finished artefacts.

| fragment | port's `t` | upstream `segmentation` (native) | what fails here |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Pot_A_Piece_04_Mesh` | +6.58 % | passes (agreement 0.9942) | `n_frac` −11.76 % |
| `Pot_B_Piece_01_Mesh` | −3.72 % | **fails** (agreement 0.9142, fracture +8.17 pp) | `fracture fraction`, `margin fraction`, `Pf spacing` |
| `Pot_G_Piece_05_Mesh_DS` | −2.27 % | passes (agreement 0.9833, fracture +0.75 pp) | `Pf spacing` 2.89× |
| `frag_010` | +4.54 % | **fails** (agreement 0.9687, fracture −2.55 pp) | `fracture fraction` −2.63 pp |

* **`Pot_A_Piece_04` is `t` and nothing else.** `n_frac ∝ A_f/t²`; the port's fracture area is
  +0.23 % and its `t` +6.58 %, so the count should move by `1.0023/1.0658² − 1 = −11.78 %`. It
  moves by −11.76 %. This is the fragment D §10.2's thickness paragraph is written about (3.554 at
  seed 0 against 3.774–3.795 at seeds 1–11; the port's 3.788 sits inside that cloud). Its spacing
  checks pass at 1.07× and no fracture sample is far from the reference's cloud.
* **`Pot_B_Piece_01` is B1's worst fragment, three times over.** Its working mesh is identical to
  the reference's entry for entry, so only the labels can differ, and they do: the port's fracture
  area is +38 % and the sampled fracture fraction +8.18 pp. The `segmentation` row measures the
  same disagreement directly as +8.17 pp — **the sampled estimate and the area measurement agree to
  0.01 pp**, which is the sampler confirming the labelling rather than a second failure. The margin
  falls by 6.63 pp because those samples were shell. 21.9 % of the port's fracture samples lie more
  than three expected spacings from the reference's fracture cloud and **100 % of them sit on faces
  the reference labels shell**; in the other direction, **zero** reference samples are far from the
  port's cloud, so the port's fracture region strictly contains the reference's — which is what a
  3.7 % smaller `t` does to R §3.4.3's shell test.
* **`frag_010`** is B1's other thickness outlier (`t` +4.54 %); it fails `fracture fraction` by
  2.63 pp where the segmentation row measures 2.55 pp, and its spacing is fine (1.03×, no far
  samples at all).
* **`Pot_G_Piece_05` is the interesting one, and it is a finding about the table rather than about
  the port.** It **passes** the segmentation row — agreement 0.9833, well inside the ±0.03 native
  gate — and still fails `Pf spacing` at 2.89×. Its working mesh is identical to the reference's,
  so nothing but the labels can differ. 581 of 12 000 fracture samples (**4.84 %**) lie more than
  three expected spacings away, and **100 % of them sit on faces the reference labels shell**; in
  the reverse direction 205 of the reference's samples are far, and 100 % of *those* sit on faces
  the port labels shell.

### 5.2 What was not done about it, and why

Nothing — the row is not widened, and the precedent is B1's and B2's. Two of the four fragments
already fail the segmentation row above; two do not, and on those the failure is arithmetic that
this note reproduces to two decimal places.

The structural point is `Pot_G_Piece_05`, and it belongs in D §13 question 2 rather than in a
tolerance. **A percentile gate on sample distances has no headroom under an area-agreement gate.**
The fracture is a tenth of the surface, so a 1.7 % disagreement of the *total* area is 14 % of the
*fracture* area, and ~5 % of the fracture samples land in it — which is exactly where a p95 sits.
There is no percentile that escapes this: at a 3 % area allowance the disagreeing share of the
fracture samples can reach a quarter. The two rows are not independent, and a tolerance-based
standard cannot separate "the port sampled differently" from "the port labelled differently" once
the labels are allowed to move at all. E6 (replicating PCG64, ≈ 2 days) removes the first half of
that question outright, and this is the third step in a row whose evidence points at it.

## 6. Cost

`sherd-refit-rs segment` runs all of R §3.1–3.7 — everything a pair needs — and the samples are
the cheapest stage in it.

| collection | Rust wall | Rust core-seconds | Python, 9 workers | speed-up (wall) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| terracotta, 4 scans (0.7–1.3 M faces) | **2.00 s** | 6.31 s | **16.83 s** | 8.4× |
| synthetic_20, 20 scans (200 k faces each) | **6.87 s** | 81.52 s | **24.52 s** | 3.6× |

The Python figure is its own preprocessing stage run exactly as `pipeline` runs it
(`ProcessPoolExecutor`, 9 workers, `_preprocess_one` per fragment, no cache), which is the 16 s the
plan quotes for the terracotta. A warm run of the Rust `segment` over the same directory is 0.01 s:
every fragment is a cache hit and nothing is recomputed.

Per fragment, of the three stages that log their own time:

| collection | segmentation | breaklines | match arrays |
|---|---|---|---|
| terracotta (4) | 0.279 s | 0.049 s | **0.0085 s** |
| synthetic_20 (20) | 2.401 s | 0.152 s | **0.0152 s** |

The sampling is 0.4–0.5 % of a fragment's work (1.58 s per terracotta fragment, 4.08 s per
synthetic one). What the rest goes on differs with the mesh: on the synthetic fragments R §3.4's
cone casts are 59 % of it, on the terracotta only 18 %, and there the reading, decimation and
Taubin of a 1.2 M-face scan dominate. Phase 1e has two different profiles to attend to, not one.

## 7. Tests (+25; 181 → 206 passing, 1 ignored)

* **The sampler** on a stretched square whose two triangles are 1:4 — 20 000 draws land 80.0 % on
  the larger one (within 1 %), every point is inside the triangle its own index names by
  barycentric coordinates, and every point stays in the plane. An empty selection and `n = 0` draw
  nothing and divide by nothing.
* **The streams**: the same seed gives the same samples, `Draw::Surface` and `Draw::Fracture` give
  different ones, the four tags are pinned and distinct, `Draw::Thickness`'s stream is
  `seeded(0)` itself, and 64 draws of the three sampling streams never coincide. The uniform is
  numpy's construction — in `[0, 1)`, an exact multiple of 2⁻⁵³, mean 0.5 over 100 000 draws.
* **The count rule** at both clamps, between them, at `int()` rather than `round()`, and at the
  `0/0` a fragment with neither fracture nor thickness would produce.
* **The margin band**: strict on both edges (a sample exactly on `0.12 t` or `1.5 t` is out), a
  fracture sample in the band is out, and a fragment with no breakline has `d_brk = ∞` and no
  margin. The thinning is sorted, seeded, and draws nothing when the margin already fits.
* **`pc_reg`'s split** keeps the proportion and the count (4 000 + 6 000 → 2 400 + 3 600), keeps
  everything below the budget and at `reg_points = 0`, and keeps one fracture point when the
  margin would otherwise take all 6 000. Rounding is numpy's half-to-even.
* **The whole of R §3.5 on a labelled square**, and on one with no fracture at all; twice with the
  same parameters, giving the same arrays.
* **`MatchData` on the real slab**: every derived array has the length its source array has, the
  margin cloud is the surface samples the index names, `pc_reg` is 6 000 points, both KD-trees
  exist — and at another `t` both halves are rebuilt (`Cow::Owned`), the margin moves with the
  band, and the surface draw does not.
* **Cache**: the sampled arrays survive the round trip bit for bit and a warm run equals a cold one
  on the real slab; a file whose `sp` does not describe its `S`, whose sample face is off the end
  of `F`, or whose `margin_idx` is off the end of `S` is refused; a cache with other `md_params`
  has its five arrays redrawn and rewritten with the mesh, the labels and the breaklines still
  coming from the cache.
* **Parity harness**: the slab dump passes the new stage in both modes (20 injected checks, 12
  native), and the point-to-triangle distance and the Poisson spacing are tested against
  hand-computed values.

`cargo fmt --all --check`, `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets --locked -- -D warnings` and
`cargo test --workspace --locked` are green. Determinism: the ignored terracotta test passes
(two `segment` runs, byte-identical caches including the five new tensors), and `--threads 1`
against `--threads 10` on the same collection gives byte-identical caches too.
