# S3 — the second placement R §5.7 threw away, the pair asked twice, and the rule that came out of the table

**Date:** 2026-09-11. **Tree:** branch `rust-core`, `10e1e02` (task S2) → `S3.1`…`S3.5`. **Machine:**
Apple M2 Pro, 10 cores (6P + 4E), 16 GB, macOS 24.6.0, rustc 1.97.0, `--release`, Python 3.12 in
`.venv` (open3d 0.19.0, numpy 2.5.2, scipy 1.18.1).

**The five sentences.** *The margin arm can now fire at all*: R §5.7 returns five candidates that
routinely converge on one fit, so `margin` was `None` for three accepted candidates in four and the
arm was decorative — the pair now records the best-scoring pose more than one wall from its own
best, taken first off R §5.6's **full** list before `keep` truncates it and, failing that, off the
stage-1 poses R §5.5 discarded, refined and scored by R §6, which gives **541 of the 1 319**
strict-clearing candidates of the 45 development runs a margin they did not have. *The pair can be
asked twice*: for every accepted pair its whole R §5–§6 search runs again on a collection drawn at
another seed and records whether its best accepted candidate lands on this placement, within 0.5 t
and 2°. *Neither witness is an arm*: a standalone re-search arm confirms **16** wrong-pose joins
over the 45 development runs (**10** if it must agree on both draws), a wide-margin arm **6**, and
the table says why — a sherd slid along a straight break is a stable optimum on every draw and
outscores every other pose of its own pair, which is the failure M1 already knew only the support
count can see. *What they are is two conjuncts on the margin arm*, and the third is the one the
table found: the second placement has to be **at least five wall thicknesses away**, because a
rival two or three walls off is the same break slid along itself and beating a slide says nothing
about which offset is right. *The rule that ships is
`support ≥ 1 OR (margin(wide) ≥ 2 over a rival ≥ 5 t away AND one re-search agrees)`*: over the 45
development runs it confirms **225 correct joins and no false one** against M1's 169 — recall
**17.4 % → 23.2 %**, above the previous step's on every set and below it at not one of the 45 seeds
— and it **removes M1's margin arm**, which is the arm every false confirmed join this project has
ever produced came out of. *And the quality gate now exits 0*: the terracotta's two museum joins are
confirmed at all five seeds, ten of ten slots, which closes audit §E's step-8 row that the gate has
failed by design since task G.

## 1. Why the margin arm could not fire

R §5.7 returns `keep = 5` candidates per pair, sorted by `seam · tight`. M1's margin is
`score / rival_score`, where the rival is the best of those five that places B more than one wall
([`SAME_PLACEMENT_T`]) from the winner. The trouble is that the five routinely **agree**: several
stage-1 poses of one pair converge on the same fit and come back as five copies of one placement.
M1 measured it as "270 of 342 correct joins make a single placement"; over the 45 runs of this step
the same fact reads:

| population | rows | with a **kept** margin | with a **wide** second placement | of those from stage 2 | from stage 1 | with a **wide** margin | **margins gained** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| every accepted candidate | 3 184 | 829 | 2 350 | 2 080 | 270 | 1 785 | **956** |
| those clearing M1's strict set | 1 319 | 241 | 943 | 731 | 212 | 782 | **541** |

*Wide second placement* counts a rival at all; *wide margin* counts the ones that **score**, so that
there is a ratio to be ahead by — a second placement whose `seam · tight` is zero is nothing to be
ahead of, which is the reading `Probes::margin` already had and the one the port keeps.

**The two places it is looked for, and why in that order.** The first is free: R §5.6 climbed and
verified the whole list and `keep` was about to throw the tail away, so the tail costs one read.
`crates/sherd-core/src/matching/pair.rs:431` takes it in the one line of `match_pair` that is not
R §5.7's, **before** `candidates.truncate(keep)`. The second is the stage-1 fallback — at most
`WIDE_STAGE1_TRIES = 4` poses R §5.5 did not keep, in R §5.4's own score order, put through
R §5.6's four rungs and scored by R §6 so that the number the margin divides by is the same quantity
as its numerator. A pose counts only if it is **still** more than one wall away *after* the ladder:
the ordinary reason a pair makes one placement is that every start converges on it, and a fallback
that read the pre-refinement distance would invent a rival out of that convergence.

Stage 2 supplies 2 080 of the 2 350 rivals and the fallback 270 — the fallback is 11 % of the
answer and it is the expensive 11 %, which is why it runs only when the free half found nothing and
only on a pair that has an accepted candidate at all.

**It is additive by construction.** `Pair::match_pair_wide` returns the list `Pair::match_pair`
returns — same poses, same order, same scores, same R §6.5 verdicts — and draws no random number;
`the_wide_rival_is_additive_and_names_a_second_placement` asserts that pose-for-pose on the
committed slab. The pipeline asks for it only when the tier pass is on, so `--tiers off` neither
pays for it nor carries it, and parity is untouched (§8).

## 2. Asking the pair again

`support` asks a **third fragment** whether it agrees with where a join puts the sherd, and a pair
with no third neighbour has no answer at all. The re-search asks the pair itself: its whole R §5–§6
search runs again on a collection whose R §3.5 samples were redrawn at `seed + RESAMPLE_OFFSETS[k]`
and whose R §5.2 coarse probe is drawn at that seed too — the frozen arithmetic, different draws —
and what is recorded is whether the re-search's own best accepted candidate lands on this placement,
inside `RESEARCH_T = 0.5 t` and `RESEARCH_DEG = 2°`.

**It borrows the stability probe's redraws rather than making its own.** Those collections already
exist for M1 §5.8's resample rows and already carry R §3.5 redrawn at the right seed; a third redraw
would cost R §3.5 over the whole collection again and buy nothing. The two probes read the same
draw for different questions: the stability rows hold the pose still and move the samples, the
re-search moves both.

**What it costs.** One whole pair match per accepted pair per seed, bounded to accepted pairs. Two
runs at seed 0, one with `--resample-seeds 0` and one with `2`, on the two sets the brief names:

| set | matching (s) | tier pass without the re-search (s) | with two re-searches (s) | the re-search adds | as a share of matching |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `synthetic_20` | 21.1 | 5.7 | 12.4 | **+6.7 s** | **+32 %** |
| `mixed_ABG` | 41.4 | 27.5 | 50.8 | **+23.3 s** | **+56 %** |

Over the whole 45-run measurement the tier pass came to 456 s against 476 s of matching (§S3
`output/s3/measure/rules.md` §5) — call it a third to a half of a run again, and it is a knob:
`--resample-seeds 0` switches it off, and `--tier-rule m1` restores the rule that never asks for it.

**One footgun, and it warns.** With the shipped rule, `--resample-seeds 0` leaves the margin arm
unable to fire — the conjunct has nothing to read — so the rule becomes `support ≥ 1` alone
(measured: `synthetic_20` seed 0 falls from 10 confirmed joins to 1). The tier pass now logs a
warning saying exactly that and naming `--tier-rule m1`.

## 3. What the two witnesses actually separate

The population that matters is the one a **second arm draws from**: candidates that clear M1's
strict set. Over the 45 runs that is 1 319 rows, 1 230 correct and **89 false** — and the false ones
are neither scattered nor varied: 69 are `pot_H`, 10 `pot_A`, 10 `mixed_ABG`, and **all 89 are
`wrong_pose` on a ground-truth-adjacent pair**. Not one is non-adjacent and not one crosses an
object. This is M1's wrong-pose family and nothing else, and the whole question of this step is
whether either new witness can see it.

| quantity | AUC over every accepted candidate | AUC over those clearing the strict set |
|---|---:|---:|
| M1's margin, off R §5.7's kept list | 0.444 | 0.598 |
| the wide margin | 0.632 | 0.650 |
| **how far the second placement is** | 0.590 | **0.806** |
| the re-search agreement | **0.833** | 0.647 |
| the support count | 0.645 | 0.666 |
| `seam` (the shared seam, for scale) | 0.853 | 0.775 |

Two readings, and they point in opposite directions.

**Reading 1 — the re-search is the best witness on the accepted population and nearly useless on
the strict one.** 0.833 falls to 0.647. The counts say it plainly: of the 89 false candidates that
clear the strict set, **79 have at least one agreeing re-search and 49 have two**. A wrong pose that
survived five strict thresholds and a slide probe is a *stable local optimum*, and moving the sample
draw does not move it. Everything the strict set already removed is what the re-search was
separating.

**Reading 2 — how far the second placement is, is the witness the strict population has.** 0.806,
and the distributions are the reason:

| population | rival distance, correct joins (min / 5 % / median / 95 % / max) | rival distance, false joins |
|---|---|---|
| every accepted candidate | 1.08 / 1.75 / 21.30 / 60.39 / 13 468 | 1.01 / 1.51 / 16.31 / 53.39 / 1 038 |
| clearing the strict set | 1.36 / 1.97 / **19.83** / 66.37 / 13 468 | 1.75 / 1.75 / **2.98** / 21.90 / 21.90 |

A correct join's best rival sits **twenty walls** away — a different fit of the same two sherds. A
wrong-pose join's sits at **three**, and three walls along a straight break is the same seam at
another offset. The failure and its signature are the same object: `pot_H`'s
`Piece_02`–`Piece_04`, the family's most persistent member, scores 17.36 with a wide rival at
**2.55 t** scoring 0.53 — a margin of 32.6 over a placement that is not a rival at all.

That is why the shipped rule asks the margin's rival to be at least `RIVAL_FAR_T = 5 t` away. It is
not a threshold fitted to a maximum; it is a statement about what a margin *means*.

dropping the re-search costs 1 — which is why both are in the rule and why neither is an arm.

### 4.1 The two clean rules at the top, and why the smaller one ships

The last two rows differ in one thing: whether M1's margin arm stays open beside the new one.

| | M1's rule | **shipped: `support ∨ (wide margin ≥ 2, rival ≥ 5 t, re-search 1)`** | the union with M1's arm |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| correct confirmed joins, 45 runs | 169 | **225** | 233 |
| false confirmed joins | 0 | **0** | 0 |
| recall of 970 adjacent pairs | 17.4 % | **23.2 %** | 24.0 % |
| joins gained against M1 | — | +64 | +64 |
| joins lost against M1 | — | **8** | 0 |
| M1's margin arm (support 0, kept margin ≥ 2) | **open** | **closed** | **open** |

The union rule has the higher recall and it is the one a literal reading of *"ship the rule with the
highest recall"* names. It is not the one that ships, and the reason is the other half of the same
brief — *precision must rise too*. **Every false confirmed join this project has ever produced came
out of M1's margin arm with support 0**: the ten on `mixed_all` (task A1 §4, task C §3), and, in
this step's own table, the six of `margin(wide)` and the sixteen of the re-search are all the same
wrong-pose family that arm cannot see. A rule that keeps that arm open cannot raise precision on the
one collection where precision is measurably wrong, whatever it does to recall. The shipped rule
closes it and replaces it with a strictly harder statement — a margin over the pair's **best**
alternative rather than over whichever of five kept poses happened to differ, that alternative
required to be a genuinely different fit, and an independent search of the pair required to land
where this one did.

**The price is eight joins, and here they are**, because a trade is a list and not a number:
`synthetic_20` s0 `frag_003–frag_018`; s1 `frag_001–frag_006`, `frag_005–frag_008`,
`frag_007–frag_009`; s2 `frag_001–frag_006`; s3 `frag_003–frag_018`; s4 `frag_001–frag_006`; and
`synthetic_mix3_24` s0 `V094_frag_000–V094_frag_005`. Seven of the eight are one set and three of
them are two pairs recurring across seeds. Each is a join M1 confirmed on the strength of "it beats
one of the five kept poses by two" and which has no independent witness of any other kind. They stay
in the **probable** band with the reason printed.

### 4.3 Colour, as the brief asked, and what it costs

Every leading rule was measured again with `colour_agreement` as an extra requirement, read as a
veto with the third state task S2 §10 says it needs: a pair with no colour abstains, **and so does a
pair whose two sherds are one flat tone** — `frac_delta_e` under 10⁻⁶ with `shell_hist` 0 is perfect
agreement produced by neither sherd saying anything, and reading that as evidence is the trap S2
measured on `pot_H`. With that reading, `… AND the clay bodies agree within 5 ΔE` changes **not one
confirmed join** on any of the nine sets, at either 5 or 7.5 ΔE. That is the expected nothing and it
is worth stating: on the development sets there are no false confirmed joins for a colour veto to
remove, and it costs nothing because it abstains wherever it has nothing to say. It is measured and
**not shipped** — a rule that changes no decision is not a rule.

### 4.4 The acceptance sets, offline: it cannot be done

The brief asks for the same table on the saved acceptance runs "where the fields can be
reconstructed". They cannot. `output/acceptance/*/seed*/report.json` carries the tier as three
counts (`confirmed`, `probable`, `rejected`) and no per-candidate evidence, and
`output/acceptance/quality/runs/*.json` carries the evaluation's join detail and no evidence either.
The wide rival and the re-search are quantities the pair search has to be **re-run** to produce, and
those runs were made before either existed. The acceptance task has to make its own runs; what this
note can hand it is the mechanism (§4.1) and the numbers above.

## 5. What ships

```
Confirmed  ⇔  R §6.5 accepted it
              ∧ tight ≥ 0.35 ∧ gap ≤ 0.015 t ∧ seam ≥ 5 t ∧ cont_n ≥ 0.90
              ∧ pen ≤ 0 (and measurable) ∧ slide ≤ 0.1 t          — M1's strict half, unmoved
              ∧ ( support ≥ 1
                  ∨ ( margin(wide) ≥ 2
                      ∧ the wide rival is ≥ 5 t away
                      ∧ ≥ 1 of 2 independent re-searches lands on this placement ) )
```

`Thresholds::S3` is that set and `Thresholds::default()` is `Thresholds::S3`. `Thresholds::M1` is
the rule in force until this step and `--tier-rule m1` restores it in one gesture; the four fields
the two rules disagree about are each a flag of its own and each overrides the preset
(`--tier-margin-rival kept|wide`, `--tier-rival-moved T`, `--tier-research N`, `--resample-seeds N`).
A tier set serialised before this step deserialises to `Thresholds::M1` exactly, which is what that
file described, and a test asserts it.

**Where the three numbers sit.** `margin(wide) ≥ 2` is on a **plateau**: 1.2 and 2 confirm the same
225 joins, 3 confirms 221 and 5 confirms 212, so 2 is M1's own number kept because nothing asks to
move it. `rival ≥ 5 t` is on a **cliff**, and the note says so plainly: at 3 t the same rule confirms
one false join, at 5 t and 8 t none — and 8 t costs 9 more correct joins (225 → 216), so five is the
first clean value and the cheapest one. `re-search ≥ 1` against `≥ 2` costs 19 correct joins for no
false one (225 → 206), so the arm asks for one; and dropping the re-search conjunct altogether buys
5 correct joins and one false one, which is the trade that put it in the rule.

**What the report says.** The confirmed table gains an `arm` column naming which of the two arms
answered for each join, and the paragraph above it is generated from the tier set in force, so a run
with `--tier-rule m1` describes M1's rule and not this one. Two more columns appear where the run
measured the thing: `margin (wide)` on any run with the tier pass on, and `re-search` (agreed / run)
on a run that performed one. The review-image caption gains `ARM`.

## 9. What is open

**1. The acceptance sets are the whole question, and this step could not touch them.** The rule was
chosen to close the arm that produced the ten false confirmed joins of `mixed_all`, and whether it
does is not measurable inside this task's scope (development collections ≤ 27 fragments). The
acceptance task should run `mixed_all` and `synthetic_mix3_60` at two seeds with the shipped rule
and with `--tier-rule m1`, and report the ten by name: under the new rule each of them needs a
margin of 2 over the pair's **best** second placement, that placement at least five walls away, and
a re-search that agrees. The mechanism says they should not survive; only the run can say they do
not. §4.4 explains why the saved acceptance runs cannot answer it offline.

**2. The re-search is the expensive half and it earns one false join.** It costs a third to a half
of the matching time (§2) and, on the development sets, its whole contribution to the *rule* is to
remove one false join at a cost of 5 correct ones (§4.1's ladder). That is a real but thin
justification, and it rests on `pot_H` — a single collection of eleven sherds. If the acceptance run
shows the re-search removing nothing on `mixed_all` either, the honest next step is to drop it and
keep the rival-distance conjunct alone: `support ∨ (margin(wide) ≥ 2 over a rival ≥ 5 t)` reaches
**230 correct joins and 1 false** on the development runs, which is more recall for one wrong-pose
join of `pot_H` — a trade a museum might prefer stated than made silently.

**3. The rival distance is a cliff and it is fitted on 89 points.** Three walls confirms one false
join, five confirms none. The population that decided it is the 89 false candidates that clear the
strict set over 45 runs, and 69 of them are one collection. `RIVAL_FAR_T = 5` is defensible as
physics — a rival inside a few walls is the same break slid along itself — but it has not been
measured on a second body of real sherds, and it should be re-read on the acceptance sets before it
is trusted as a constant rather than as this project's current best guess.

**4. `seam` separates the wrong-pose family better than either new witness, reaches a higher recall
than the shipped rule, and is not shipped.** This is the uncomfortable row of the whole step and it
belongs in the open list, not buried. `seam` — the length of the shared seam in wall thicknesses —
has AUC **0.853** on the accepted population and **0.775** on the strict one (§3), better than the
wide margin, the re-search or the support count. Added to M1's rule as a bare third arm on all 45
runs it gives:

| third arm | correct | false |
|---|---:|---:|
| `seam ≥ 42 t` | **246** | 0 |
| `seam ≥ 45 t` | 241 | 0 |
| `seam ≥ 50 t` | 236 | 0 |

against the shipped rule's 225 and the union rule's 233. Three reasons it is not the rule, and the
first is decisive. **(a)** It keeps M1's margin arm open — every row above is *M1's rule plus* — so
it cannot raise precision on `mixed_all`, which is the half of the brief this step exists for.
**(b)** 41.0 t is the largest seam any of the 89 false candidates has, so `42` sits **one wall above
the observed maximum**: no headroom at all, on 89 points of which 69 are one pot. **(c)** `seam`
scales with how big a sherd is; the development collections are a handful of vessels of a few sizes,
and a museum collection of larger sherds would move the whole distribution under the threshold.
Choosing a constant that way is how the ten false joins of `mixed_all` were produced in the first
place. It is written down in full because the next person looking for recall will find it again and
should find this paragraph with it — and because if the acceptance run gives it a second body of
negatives to be measured against, it may well be right.

**5. The union rule is measured and not reachable by a flag.** `M1's rule ∨ (the new arm)` confirms
233 correct joins and loses nothing (§4.1). It is not shipped because it keeps M1's margin arm open,
and it is not behind a flag because a fifth knob for a rule the step argues against would be a knob
nobody should turn. `--tier-rule m1 --tier-research 1` is *not* it; there is no flag combination that
produces it, and if the acceptance run shows M1's arm is safe after all, the right change is to make
it one.

**6. Colour still reads nothing.** §4.3 measured every leading rule with the clay-body veto and it
changes not one confirmed join on any development set, at 5 or 7.5 ΔE. Task S2's §10 stands
unchanged: the collections that have colour have no false joins to remove, and the collection that
has false joins has no colour.

**7. Two smaller things.** `WIDE_STAGE1_TRIES = 4` was chosen by argument and not by measurement —
the fallback supplies 270 of the 2 350 rivals and nobody has asked what 2 or 8 would supply. And the
re-search reads only whether the redrawn search *agrees*; it also records the score and the distance
of the placement it found, and neither is in any rule.
