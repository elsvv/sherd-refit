## 1. What was ported

`crates/sherd-core/src/assembly/` is R §8 and R §8.2 and nothing else.

| module | R | what it holds |
|---|---|---|
| `groups.rs` | §8, §8.2 | `Piece` — the assembly's whole view of a fragment; `Grouping` — the reference's `poses`, `group_of` and `groups` while they grow, with `thickness`, the seed, the singletons and the stable sort by size; `recenter` |
| `consistency.rs` | §8 | `penetration` (R §6.4's count at the pair's own `pen`), `disagreement` (`rotation_angle_deg` and `‖τ‖ / tg`), the `10° / 0.5 t` tolerances |
| `greedy.rs` | §8 | `best_per_pair`, `try_place`, the loop, `Rejection` with the reference's four sentences, `Assembly` |
| `constraints.rs` | roadmap item 3 | `{must_join, must_not_join}` typed; **nothing reads it** |

`Piece` is data rather than a `&Fragment` on purpose: R §8 asks three things of a fragment (how
thick is its wall, how coarse is its mesh, is that mesh closed), moves one point set through one
pose and casts against one BVH. Taking those five fields lets the parity harness build a `Piece`
out of the *dump's* arrays, which is the whole of D §10.2's injected column here — and it puts
PMC-8 in exactly one field, `s_pen`, so the choice belongs to the caller and no test below knows
which sample set it got.

Two small things moved in `sherd-core` to avoid a second copy of arithmetic the pair stages already
verified:

* `matching::verify::pose_inverse` is public. R §8 calls `np.linalg.inv` four times per placement
  (`rel`, `poses[other]⁻¹`, `T_alt⁻¹`, and once inside the penetration test) and PMC-19 is the row
  that says what that factorisation is; there is one implementation of it.
* `matching::verify::penetration_share` is R §6.4's count in one direction, without the second
  pass `one_penetration` makes to find `min(sd)` when nothing is inside — R §8 never reads
  `pen_depth`. The per-point predicate is factored out (`depth_inside`) so the two share it exactly;
  the share is spread over `rayon`, which cannot change a count. `types::apply_transform` is the
  reference's `apply_transform` for one point, which `verify.rs` now calls instead of repeating.

## 2. Four tie rules R §8's pseudocode leaves to the reader

None changes what the reference computes. Each is a place where a plain transcription of R §8
produces a different assembly, and all four are now in R §12.1 as well.

1. **`best_per_pair` keeps on a strict `>`**, so the *first* candidate at a pair's top score
   represents it; and `accepted`'s stable sort is over `dict.values()`, i.e. **insertion order**, so
   two pairs of equal score come back in the matcher's order (R §4.1's `itertools.combinations`).
   The port tracks that insertion order explicitly rather than letting a map decide it.
2. **The pass iterates a snapshot.** `for c in list(remaining)` in the Python: a join removed during
   a pass does not shorten that pass. Measured: walking the live list instead — the natural
   transcription — changes the answer on **four of the eight sets** (pot_A fails 3 of its 6 injected
   rows, pot_B 1, pot_H 1, synthetic_20 5; the slab, the terracotta, pot_C and pot_G are unaffected).
3. **The consistency loop walks all of `accepted`**, not what is left of `remaining`, and `c2 is c`
   is an identity test on the object. The port compares positions in `accepted`, which is the same
   thing and cannot be confused with a candidate that happens to have equal fields.
4. **R §8.2's centroid is over the concatenation** of every member's `S[::10]`, not the mean of the
   members' means. They coincide while every fragment carries the same `surface_points`, which is
   true on both sides today, and the port takes the concatenation so that it stays true if that
   changes. The sum is `pairwise_sum`, because `pts.mean(0)` is numpy's pairwise reduction.

**A fifth finding: R §8's `would merge two groups (not supported)` branch cannot fire.** A second
group is seeded only after a whole pass has made no progress, and such a pass has scanned and
removed *every* join with exactly one placed endpoint; what remains all has both endpoints unplaced,
so it touches no existing group, and the first group is frozen from that moment. By induction no
join ever has its two endpoints in two different groups. The dumps agree: **none of the eight** has
a rejection of that kind. The port keeps the branch — it is R §8's text, and R §8.1's second pass
reruns the loop on another candidate list — and its test exercises the *sentence* rather than the
path.

**Why the pose rows have a tolerance when the other three do not.** A world pose is a product of up

the two poses give the fragment's *own* surface samples (every tenth), in `t` — not the displacement
of the origin, which a 1e-16 rotation difference 450 units out inflates. Worst anywhere: **9.1e-14 t**
before recentring, **7.0e-13 t** after the reference's own refinement and the port's recentring.

**The `recentre` row is end to end and it is the only row that reads `outputs/`.** The pipeline
recentres *after* R §9, so the row feeds the port's `recenter` the reference's own
`refine/poses_final.json` under the dump's own grouping and compares against
`outputs/transforms.json`. On pot_G, where no group has two members and R §9 never ran,
`assembly/poses.json` is the input instead — which makes that set a direct check of R §8 followed
by R §8.2 with nothing in between.

**Three of synthetic_20's 190 pairs have no `result.candidates.json`, and that is R §5 rather than
a truncated dump.** `_match_pair` returns `[]` at three points *before* the sink is written: no
fracture sample or no breakline, no hypothesis, or nothing above R §5.3's floor. `frag_003__frag_005`,
`frag_004__frag_014` and `frag_004__frag_015` are the third case — `nms1.kept` is written and empty,
their best coarse score being 0.067–0.083 against the floor of 0.1 — so they contributed nothing to
the reference's own `cands`. The harness reads each exit's own evidence and treats the pair as
candidate-free; with none of the three present it skips the collection instead, which is what a
`min`-level dump would look like.

What moves is the *number inside* a `penetrates` sentence, because that number is R §6.4's fraction

## 6. synthetic_20 is four joins short, and the reason is a hard threshold

The port's own assembly on synthetic_20 is `[15, 3, 1, 1]` where the reference's is `[19, 1]`. It
accepts 23 joins, as the reference does, and then refuses four of them — three of which the
reference used, and one (`frag_005-frag_007`) that only the port accepted:

```
frag_010-frag_015: penetrates frag_002 (0.012)
frag_002-frag_015: penetrates frag_010 (0.006)
frag_016-frag_019: penetrates frag_000 (0.012)
frag_005-frag_007: inconsistent with the assembled poses (0.5 deg, 0.77 t)
```

`max_pen` is **0.005**, so the three penetrations are 120, 240 and 240 samples of 20 000 against a
budget of 100. **This is not the assembly disagreeing.** On the *reference's* poses, with the port's
own samples, the port finds no penetration on synthetic_20 at all — that is the `pmc8 rejected` row
of §4, which is identical to the reference's empty list. What differs is where the port's own search
puts these three sherds, and C3 measured that: over the twenty joins both sides accept on this set,
the two placements differ by 0.0096 t at the median and **0.065 t at the maximum**. A sherd resting
0.065 t further into its neighbour puts one to two samples per thousand past a `pen` depth of
0.06 t, and 0.005 is the line.

The fourth rejection is the same story through the other test: a loop-closing edge at 0.5° and
**0.77 t**, against R §8's `0.5 t`.

So the port's synthetic_20 assembly is not wrong in R §8's terms — every rejection is R §8's own
rule applied to the port's own poses — and it is four joins worse in the terms R §13 cares about.
The two levers it is sensitive to are both above this stage: R §5.3's tie-break (PMC-6) and R §3.5's
sampler (PMC-9). The benchmark step will say what it costs in fragment accuracy; the number to
watch is that R §13 asks for 95 % on this set.

## 7. Cost

The port's `assemble` alone — the whole loop, penetration tests included — against the reference's
own `assemble` on the same candidate lists. Both sides best of three on an **idle** machine, the
reference with OpenMP free, which is what its pipeline gives the assembly (only its preprocessing
and matching workers are pinned, `pipeline.py::_worker_env`).

| set | reference `assemble` | + its `t_med` rebuild | port | factor |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| pot_H (11 fragments, 16 accepted joins) | 0.526 s | 0.36 s | **0.027 s** | 19× (33× with the rebuild) |
| synthetic_20 (20 fragments, 23 accepted joins) | 3.453 s | 6.11 s | **0.048 s** | 72× (199× with the rebuild) |

The rebuild column is what PMC-8 removes: the reference builds a second `MatchData` per fragment at
`t_med` for this stage alone — 6.1 s on synthetic_20, more than the assembly itself — and the port
reads the cached one.

**The factor is much larger than anywhere else in this port, and it is PMC-11's formulation rather
than the language.** R §8 asks the penetration question of every *pair of group members*, and most
pairs of sherds in an assembled pot do not overlap at all: the port's `penetration_share` answers
those with one AABB test per point, no ray and no distance query, while Open3D's
`compute_signed_distance` runs a closest-triangle search for all 15 000 points whatever the
geometry. The number is a property of the question R §8 asks, not a Rust-against-Python ratio —
D §10.2's **7.2× at one thread and 8.8× at ten** for a whole `match_pair` is the figure to quote
for that.

**The first measurement of this taken was wrong by a factor of two, on a busy machine** (pot_H read
1.170 s against these 0.526 s, synthetic_20 6.500 s against 3.453 s) — defect D11's lesson applied
to this step's own numbers. Everything in this table is idle-machine.

For scale, the stage is negligible next to the pair matching it sits on: the port's whole native
`assembly` run over synthetic_20 — preprocess 20 fragments, match 190 pairs, assemble twice — takes
73 s, of which the two assemblies are **0.083 s**.

## 8. Determinism

`assemble` is index-ordered end to end — `remaining` and `accepted` are `Vec<usize>`, the group
membership is a `Vec<FragId>` in placement order, and the only parallelism is the point loop inside
`penetration_share`, whose answer is a count. Checked rather than argued: the injected `assembly`
stage on pot_H is **byte-identical across two runs and between one and ten threads**
(`RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1`), and the native stage on pot_C is byte-identical between one, two and ten,
timestamps aside.

## 9. Tests (+16; 266 → 282 passing, 1 ignored)

* `crates/sherd-core/tests/assembly_graphs.rs` (7) — hand-made candidate graphs, one per branch of
  the loop: a **chain** that must grow from its strongest join and compose the poses; a **triangle**
  whose third edge closes a loop it disagrees with (rejected, and the fragment stays where the chain
  put it) and the same triangle with an edge that agrees (used); **two objects** and a fragment
  belonging to neither, which must come back as three groups, biggest first; a **penetrating join**
  against a group member that is not its anchor, followed by the weaker join that then proposes the
  same fragment elsewhere and is refused against it — the only route to
  `inconsistent with stronger join`, since a stronger join is otherwise tried first; the two tie
  rules of §2.1; and R §8.2 on three cubes. The penetration test's interior points are deliberately
  irregular: an axis-aligned ray from a point whose coordinates are equal or sum to one leaves a
  cube through a triangulation diagonal, where a crossing count is not a function of the geometry,
  and a cube can present that on two of `RayScene::inside`'s three rays at once.
* `crates/sherd-core/src/assembly/{groups,consistency,constraints}.rs` (7) — the group thickness and
  its `+ [new]`, the singleton order and the stable sort, R §8.2 on a stride, the two tolerances and
  their inclusivity, the clip that stops a trace of `1 + 4e-14` returning `NaN`, and the constraint
  file's unordered pairs and defaults.
* `crates/sherd-parity/tests/stages_slab.rs` (2) — the row made to fail by exactly the thing it
  measures (a split group, a missing used join, an invented rejection — each failing its own row and
  no other), and a dump with no `assembly/md_t_median.json`, which skips rather than comparing
  against nothing.
