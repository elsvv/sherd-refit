## 1. What was ported

**`icp.rs` — R §7's `registration_icp`, and nothing else.** It is a transcription of Open3D rather
than an implementation of ICP: the loop applies each update to the *already transformed* source
cloud instead of recomputing it from the source, the returned `fitness` and `inlier_rmse` are the
ones measured **after** the last update, and the convergence test is Open3D's
(`|Δfitness| < 1e-6 ∧ |Δrmse| < 1e-6`, checked after the search that follows an update, so a rung
that converges still applied the update that converged it). Both estimators are R §7's: Umeyama
without scaling for point-to-point, and for point-to-plane the 6×6 normal equations, a pivoted
LDLT and the `Rz(x₂)·Ry(x₁)·Rx(x₀)` Euler composition — not the exponential map, which R §7 says
is not equivalent for a single iteration.

**`ladder.rs` — the two ladders.** Stage 1 is two point-to-point rungs of `B.pc_brk` against
`A.pc_brk_full` at `sc.icp_dist(0.2)` and `sc.icp_dist(0.08)`, 20 iterations each, followed by
R §5.4's re-score; stage 2 is four point-to-plane rungs, `pc_reg` at 0.2 t and 0.08 t and then
`pc_frac` at 0.08 t and 0.04 t, 30 iterations each. The radii are `k · t_pair · sc.icp`, so the
`Scales` stretch of R §1.2 scales the whole ladder together rather than flooring each rung
separately — a coarse mesh cannot make the fine rung overtake the coarse one. `climb` returns
**every** rung's result, not only the last, because the dump carries a pose per rung and a ladder
that arrives at the right pose by a different route is a different ladder.

R §5.4's re-score is not the ICP's own `fitness`. It is the coarse estimator of R §5.2 at the
tighter `sc.stage1` radius over the whole of B's `brk_sub`, normal test included, which is why
`coarse.rs` grew `score_pose`: same kernel, another radius and another point set, written once.

**`pair.rs`** gained `stage1`, `suppress_stage1` and `stage2` on top of C1's state, and the parity
harness gained the two rows and a shared `clouds` reader that rebuilds R §3.6's four clouds from
the dump's own `Pf`, `S`, `fp`, `sp`, `margin_idx` and `brk_*` arrays. `samples::registration_split`
is R §3.6's prefix split, made public so that the harness splits `pc_reg` the way the pipeline
does instead of writing the rule twice.

## 2. Open3D's ICP, measured rather than assumed

Five details decide whether a pose comes out the same. Four were checked against Open3D 0.19
directly rather than read out of its source.

**The correspondence radius is a strict `<`.** Open3D hands FLANN `max_correspondence_distance²`
and FLANN drops a candidate at `dist >= radius`. A single source point at distance `d` from its
only target, with `max_correspondence_distance = d`:

| source at | Open3D `fitness` | correspondences |
|---|---|---|
| one ULP inside `d` | 1.0 | 1 |
| exactly `d` | 0.0 | 0 |
| one ULP outside `d` | 0.0 | 0 |

The port therefore compares squared distances and rejects `d² >= r²` — and `PointTree` grew
`nearest_within_squared` so that the search returns `d²` rather than `sqrt(d²)`, which the caller
would have had to square again. Two roundings the reference does not have.

**`fitness` divides by the number of source points, `inlier_rmse` by the number of
correspondences.** Three source points, two of them inside the radius at 0.1 and 0.4: Open3D
returns `fitness = 0.6666666666666666` (2/3, not 2/2) and `inlier_rmse = 0.29154759474226505`,
which is `sqrt((0.1² + 0.4²)/2)` to the last bit. The error is a sum of *squared* distances,
never re-derived from a square root.

**The summation order is the source order.** Open3D accumulates the correspondence set, the
squared error and `ComputeJTJandJTr` under OpenMP, merging thread-private partials in whatever
order the threads reach the critical section. The fixtures were dumped at `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`
(D §10.1, the P0 note), which makes that a plain sequential sum in ascending source index, and
that is what the port does on every thread count (D §7). The effect is visible: re-running the
reference on the same pair with ten threads instead of one moves the seven well-conditioned
candidates of `Pot_B_Piece_01__06` by up to 8.5e-10 t — and the other three by 10–22°, which is
§5.

**The point-to-plane update composes Euler angles.** R §7 states it and the port follows it; the
rungs of §4 are the evidence that nothing else would have fitted.

**Umeyama's `1/n` is a multiplication.** Eigen computes `one_over_n` once and multiplies the row
sums by it, which is not the same double as dividing by `n` — the same shape of detail as C1's
`k/60`. The covariance's `1/n` is folded into each term here where Eigen hands it to the matrix
product as `alpha`; that difference cannot reach the result by more than rounding, because `Σ` and
`cΣ` have the same singular vectors for `c > 0` and only `U` and `Vᵀ` leave the function. The
stage-1 rows agree: 0.000° at the median over all 71 841 candidates.

## 5. The 48 candidates neither implementation can reproduce

Some candidates' ladders are not functions of their input at double precision, and the harness
refuses to compare those rather than widening a tolerance to swallow them. A candidate that fails
its row is re-run from the twelve initial poses one ULP away (each entry of the 3×4 block moved to
its next representable neighbour, one at a time); if any of those answers lands outside the row's
own tolerance of this one, the candidate is *not determined* and is counted in a `chaotic` row
instead of a pose row. The probe costs nothing on a candidate that passed.

That is the port's own test, so it proves nothing about the reference. What follows does. On
`Pot_B_Piece_01__06`, whose ten stage-2 candidates start from the dump's own `s1.T` and are
therefore bit-exact inputs, Open3D was re-run on the same clouds — the dump's own arrays — at
`OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`:

| candidate | reproduces its own dumped pose | one-ULP spread of Open3D's answer | perturbations that moved it | ten threads instead of one |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| 0, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 | exactly | 0.0000° / 0.0000 t | 0 of 12 | ≤ 8.5e-10 t |
| 1 | exactly | 65.5° / 131 t | 11 of 12 | 10.4° / 8.2 t |
| 2 | exactly | 101.7° / 145 t | 12 of 12 | 22.1° / 34.5 t |
| 3 | exactly | 24.8° / 39.4 t | 12 of 12 | 11.4° / 11.4 t |

Candidates 1, 2 and 3 are exactly the three the port's probe refuses on that pair. The reference
reproduces itself perfectly when nothing changes and lands a hundred degrees away when the last
bit of its input changes, or when the same binary reduces its sums in a different order. No
implementation can follow that, and a comparison of two implementations there measures the last
bit of a reduction.

Stage 1 behaves the same way for the same reason. On terracotta `FY234021__FY234104` the ladder of
candidate 165 keeps **thirteen** correspondences on the first rung and **six** on the second: a
Umeyama fit on six points of a curve. Open3D's own answer for it moves by 177.4° / 3.58 t on five
of the twelve single-ULP perturbations, and running the same Open3D on ten threads moves it by the
same 177.4°, while candidates 0, 1 and 2 of the same pair reproduce to 7e-15 t and 3e-6° at both
thread counts (this ladder starts from a hypothesis pose rebuilt from the dump's frames rather than
from a dumped matrix, so its input is the reference's to within an ulp rather than exactly).

The counts, and the row that keeps them honest:

| stage | candidates | not determined | worst set | worst pair | reached the next stage |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|
| stage 1 | 71 841 | 11 (1.5e-4) | terracotta, 2 of 1 459 (1.4e-3) | 1 of 30 (0.033) | **0** |
| stage 2 | 2 249 | 37 (1.6e-2) | pot B, 12 of 360 (0.033) | 3 of 10 (0.30) | **0** |

The last column is the point. `chaotic kept` requires that every stage-1 candidate excused here was
one the reference's own R §5.5 discarded, and `chaotic accepted` that every stage-2 candidate
excused was one the reference's own verification refused; both are exact rows and both are zero on
all eight sets. Every one of the 37 stage-2 cases is refused with `tight = 0` and `seam = 0` — no
contact and no seam at all, which is what a ladder that walked out of contact produces — while the
joins survive: the one accepted candidate of `Pot_B_Piece_01__07` (tight 0.312, seam 22.7) is not
among the three the probe refuses there. **A pose that the reference went on to use is never
excused**, and if one ever were, the exact row would fail.

The gates are `chaotic ≤ 0.002` per dump and `≤ 0.06` per pair for stage 1, `≤ 0.06` and `≤ 0.4`
for stage 2 — the measured worst plus headroom, in the shape of the PMC-6 rows of the `nms` stage.
They are alarms, not parity claims: their job is to fail if a future change makes the port
chaotic where the reference is not.

## 6. Experiment E5 — `f32` point loops, and D §7's centred assembly

D §3's E5 asks whether the ICPs can run in `f32`, and expects the answer to be worth 1e-5 t.
`--icp-precision f32` runs the point loops — the transform of the moving cloud, the residual, the
Jacobian row and its accumulation — in `f32` with the pose and the 6×6 solve still `f64`, over
target clouds narrowed to `f32` and back, which is the arithmetic D §6.5's GPU kernels would do.
`--icp-assembly centred` assembles the normal equations about the target centroid and re-expresses
the update about the origin, which is D §7's remedy for ill-conditioning.

**Neither is inside D §10.2, and both are worth the measurement.**

`f32`, injected, against the reference's poses (translations in wall thicknesses):

| pot A | 3.56 | 1.72 | 7.9e-5° / 1.2e-4 | 0.045° / 0.055 | 0.088° / 0.125 | 95.9° / 124 |
| pot B | 3.46 | 1.75 | 8.0e-5° / 1.2e-4 | 0.123° / 0.164 | 0.083° / 0.115 | 165° / 752 |
| pot C | 5.83 | 2.98 | 5.2e-5° / 6.5e-5 | 4.4e-4° / 6.6e-4 | 1.93° / 2.33 | 169° / 989 |

The pattern is not subtle and it is not about the ladder: `f32` has ~1e-7 relative precision, the
scans live 100–600 units from their own origin, so an `f32` coordinate is good to about 1e-5
units — and what that is *worth* depends on `t`. On terracotta, `t = 38.7`, it is 3e-7 t and the
median candidate is fine. On pot G, `t = 2.36` and the resolution floor has stretched the ladder by
3.8, it is 4e-6 t of a much smaller wall and the **median** stage-2 candidate lands 9.8 t away. A
correspondence set that changes by one point near a radius boundary changes the trajectory, and
thirty iterations later the two poses are unrelated.

The stage-1 ladders survive better than stage 2 in the bulk of the distribution — p99 is inside
0.01 t on five of the seven sets and inside 0.05° on six — because a point-to-point rung on a
breakline is a 20-iteration fit whose radius is wide relative to the point spacing, so a
correspondence rarely changes. Stage 2's four rungs on 6 000 and 12 000 surface points, at radii
down to 0.04 t, change correspondences constantly. The tail is bad in both: stage 1's maximum is
121–162° on terracotta and pot C, on the candidates §5 already calls chaotic — in `f32` the
`chaotic` row does not fire for them, because the probe re-runs the ladder in `f32` too and finds
it locally stable while it sits somewhere else entirely.

E5's conclusion: **the GPU executor of D §6.5 cannot run these ladders in `f32`.** The design's
1e-5 t was optimistic by four orders of magnitude on the thin-walled pots, which are the sets the
project cares about; even synthetic 20, the most forgiving set after terracotta, has a stage-2
median of 0.0075 t against a 0.01 t tolerance and a p99 of 34.7 t. What could be salvaged is
stage 1's point-to-point rungs, and that is not enough to matter: stage 2 is where the time goes.

`Assembly::Centred` in `f64` is the second half of E5 and the more interesting failure. It is an
exact change of variables of the *linear* Gauss–Newton step — `ω` is unchanged and
`τ = τ' + (I − R)c` — but the finite update is not the same: `World` composes `p ↦ Rp + τ` and
`Centred` composes `p ↦ c + R(p − c) + τ'`, and the two differ by `(R − I − ω̂)c = O(|ω|²·|c|)` per
iteration. D §7 estimated that at 1e-6 t. Measured on terracotta, stage 2:

| | p50 | p90 | p99 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rotation | 0.473° | 7.47° | 14.4° | 15.6° |
| translation | 0.044 t | 0.61 t | 1.39 t | 1.57 t |

Which is what `O(|ω|²·|c|)` actually comes to with these constants: the terracotta fracture clouds
sit `|c| ≈ 100–150` scan units from the origin (2.5–3.8 t), and the first `pc_reg` rung takes
`|ω| ≈ 0.1` rad steps for thirty iterations without converging, so the per-iteration difference is
≈ 0.03 t and the rung ends 0.044 t out at the median. Stage 1 is unaffected — point-to-point never
assembles normal equations — and the unit test that has both assemblies converging on the same
answer is a *converged* case, where `ω → 0` takes the term with it.

The knob stays, because it is the instrument that measured this and because D §7 names it. What it
is not is a drop-in for the GPU path: the way to shrink coordinates for `f32` is to translate
**both clouds** by `−c` and run R §7 verbatim in that frame — a rigid change of frame the whole of
R §7 is equivariant under — rather than to re-parameterise the Jacobian alone. That is a change to
D §7's row, and it belongs to whoever writes the GPU executor.

Both ladders of one pair: 0.695 s against 7.03 s on the terracotta pair and 0.731 s against 3.87 s
on the pot B pair, single-threaded, so **5.3–10.1×**. One thread of the port also beats ten of the
reference by 2.4–6.6×. Part of that is the language and part of it is structural: Open3D builds a
`KDTreeFlann` inside every `registration_icp` call — forty tree builds over 6 000 and 12 000 points
for one pair's stage 2 — while the port builds one tree per cloud and reuses it across the rungs
and across the pair's candidates.

The whole injected comparison, which additionally re-registers the reference's own pose for the
`fitness`/`rmse` rows, runs both stages of a set on ten cores in 0.84 s (terracotta, 6 pairs) to
20.0 s (synthetic 20, 190 pairs); pot H's 55 pairs take 11.0 s.

R §13's own profile of a mid-size pair puts stage 2's rungs at 2.84 s (the two `pc_reg` ones) plus
1.82 s (the two `pc_frac` ones) = 4.66 s, 67 % of that pair's 6.94 s. That sits between the two
pairs measured here (6.22 s and 1.71 s single-threaded), and the port's equivalents are 0.617 s and
0.387 s — which moves the same 67 % to roughly the same share of a much smaller total.

## 8. Tests (+11; 234 → 245 passing, 1 ignored)

`icp.rs` carries seven: point-to-point recovers a known rigid transform to 1e-9° and 1e-9 units in
three iterations with `fitness = 1`; point-to-plane on a plane pair converges onto the plane to
1e-9 while leaving the three degrees of freedom the geometry does not constrain alone (which is the
rank-3 system Eigen's pivoted LDLT survives and a plain Cholesky does not); a narrower radius keeps
fewer correspondences and the ladder still lands on the truth; the correspondence radius is strict
at exactly `d` and one ULP either side; an empty correspondence set, an empty cloud and a
non-positive radius all return the pose unmoved rather than dividing by zero; the numerics knobs
move a converged pose by far less than the gate; and the Euler composition is `Rz·Ry·Rx` with a
