# O1: object separation — what a group of sherds says about itself, and what it is allowed to decide

**Date:** 2026-09-09. **Tree:** branch `rust-core`, previous step `e4399fd` (T2).
**Step:** 10 of the audit's amended plan (`notes/2026-09-09-fable-audit.md` §E; D §12 row 10).
**What it is:** roadmap item 4. Every fragment carries audit §D.2's feature table from
preprocessing and the cache carries it; every assembled group is reported as an **object** with the
consensus its members agree on and the members that consensus does not fit; a **confirmed** join
between two groups may merge them; `same_object` and `different_object` are read. `--objects off`
reproduces T2's outputs byte for byte.

**The one-line result:** on the shipped settings the object pass **changes no decision on any of the
eight development sets** — 40 runs, 1 125 scored cells, 0 differ against T2's table — and adds a
section a conservator can read. That is not a disappointment; it is what task M1 §4's measurement
predicted, and this note is mostly the evidence for why each of audit §D.2's three arms is where it
is.

## 1. The features are part of R §3 now, and the cache carries them

Audit §D.2 asks for `Features` "per fragment, at preprocessing, stored in the cache
(`CACHE_VERSION` 6)", and that is what `CACHE_VERSION` 6 is. D §4.2 sketched them as optional
`features/*` **tensors**; what they turned out to be is thirteen numbers, a name, a flag and two Lab
triples, which belong in the metadata block beside `thick` rather than in a tensor of length one.

**The numbers are M1's numbers.** `segment --features` on `pot_C` now writes the fragments' own
stored table rather than a second computation of it, and it reproduces
`output/measure/features/pot_C.json` **row for row and field for field** — the seven fragments, the
seventeen fields each. That includes the colour, which is the one that could have moved: M1 measured
it over the mesh as the file spells it, and R §3.1's `clean` merges duplicate vertices before
anything else sees them. So preprocessing takes the colour through a new hook,
`io::load_mesh_with`, which is `load_mesh` with the raw mesh handed to a closure before the clean —
one implementation, so the two cannot drift.

**The seed moves the geometry and not the fabric.** Every geometric feature is measured on R §3.5's
draw, so a cache read back at another seed has its samples redrawn and its features with them; the
four colour fields are carried across instead, because the fabric is a property of the file and
re-reading a 500 000-vertex scan to learn it again would be the most expensive thing a warm run
did. `the_object_features_are_cached_with_the_fragment_and_redrawn_with_its_samples` asserts both
halves.

**Cost.** 0.15 s of `mixed_ABG`'s 3.2 s cold preprocessing — 24 fragments, `--no-cache`, measured
against the same pass without `--features` — and **nothing at all on a warm cache**. The Lab
transform is memoised per distinct RGB triple, which is what keeps a museum scan with 30 000
distinct colours over half a million vertices from paying for half a million `cbrt`s; the
accumulation order is the vertex order either way, so the sums are the same bits.

**The one field this moves in an output.** `report.json`'s `engine.cache_version` reads 6 where it
read 5. That is forced by the brief's own instruction and it is the only byte outside the object
section and block that this step changes; §5's identity check excludes it by name, beside
`engine.commit`.

## 2. What acts, what reports, and why each is where it is

Audit §D.2 gives roadmap item 4 three arms. Two of them ship inert and one ships on, and every one
of those three positions is a measurement.

### 2.1 The consensus reports, because no feature earned a veto

Audit §D.2's own rule: a feature may **veto** only where its measured AUC exceeds **0.800**. Task
M1 §4 measured every one of them on every collection whose object ids are real — `mixed_ABG` (the
development test), `mixed_all` (the acceptance set) and the pooled `pots_A_H` — and the best is
**0.740** (`shell_radius` on `mixed_ABG`), then 0.695, 0.694 and 0.663. So `ObjectParams::demote`
ships **empty**: `GroupFeatures` is computed, printed per object and drawn in the review image, and
it demotes nothing.

The mechanism is there and it is off, which is not the same thing as absent.
`--object-demote shell_radius` is one flag away, and §6 says what the note that turns it on will
have to carry.

**A consensus of two is a subtraction, not a spread.** A member is measured against its object
*without itself* — "does this sherd belong to this pot" is a question about the other members — and
the terracotta measured what happens when "the other members" are two of them: its four scans have
walls 38.577, 38.808, 39.024 and 40.177, and against a two-member median the fourth reads
**11.7 MAD** out. Every collection of three would report an outlier. `MIN_CONSENSUS_MEMBERS` is
therefore **3** and it is a rule about the consensus, so an object needs four members before it can
reject one. With it, the terracotta reports no outlier, which is the right answer for a collection
that is one pot.

A MAD of zero refuses the question rather than answering infinity — which is M1 §4's `lab_a` and
`lab_b` on every SfS++ collection, one flat grey per file.

### 2.2 Audit §D.2 (b)'s mutual-disagreement arm ships **off**, and this is the measurement

The arm is implemented exactly as §D.2 (b) words it: *"when two accepted joins into one group
disagree about a fragment, demote both unless one is confirmed"*, at R §8's own tolerances (10°,
0.5 `t` of the group's wall) and over the joins the assembly's gate admits.

**How "unless one is confirmed" is read.** Two joins into one group that contradict each other are
evidence that at least one is wrong and nothing in R §6 says which. When exactly one is confirmed
the contradiction is explained by the weaker one; when **both** are, there is nothing to choose
between them and both fall to probable. Read the other way — "either of them is confirmed" — the
rule fires only when both are probable already, and demoting a probable join to probable is
nothing: the sentence would be inert. So the reading above is the only one under which §D.2 (b)
does anything.

**And what it does is remove correct joins.** Two full gate runs, forty runs each:

**False joins in the confirmed tier: 0 with the arm off, 0 with it on.** There were none to catch —
task T1 measured zero over these same forty runs — so the arm is a pure recall loss here: it costs
**60 of 136 correct confirmed joins, 44 %**, and buys nothing measurable. Every contradiction it
finds is between joins R §8 has already reconciled: R §8 refuses the odd one out itself, with
`inconsistent with the assembled poses` (20 such refusals over the forty runs), and the arm then
demotes the two it kept as well.

So `--object-disagreement` defaults to **off**, exactly as task T1 shipped `--tier-resample-accept`
and `--tier-max-determined-deg` off for the same kind of reason: an arm that cannot be shown to
remove a false join is a threshold fitted to nothing. The flag is there, the code is tested, and
the day a false join survives the tier is the day to measure it again.

*(This is a departure from the brief's default, and it is the audit's own methodology: "thresholds
are chosen on the measurement table, not guessed".)*

### 2.3 The demotion is per **pair**, not per candidate

One place where this reads a band differently from task T1. A pair routinely has several candidates
converging on one placement and all of them confirmed — 44 candidates for 11 pairs on `mixed_ABG`
seed 0, measured — so demoting only the representative would leave R §8's `best_per_pair` free to
pick the next candidate of the same pair and place the join anyway. It was measured doing exactly
that before the fix. What the arm says is about the two sherds, so it is said about the pair.

A demotion never rejects: [`Tier::Confirmed`] → [`Tier::Probable`] and no further, which is
audit §D.2's own *"demoted to probable, **not rejected** — the conservator decides"*.

### 2.4 One round, not a fixed point

`object_round` finds the demotions on the assembly R §8 has just built, applies them, and runs R §8
**once** more. Not to a fixed point: a second round's demotions would be computed on the groups the
first round's demotions built, and the answer would then depend on how many rounds were run. With
the shipped defaults nothing is ever demoted, so the second assembly never runs and the pass costs
**0.001 s** of `mixed_ABG`'s 61.5 s (against `tiers` 23.3 s and `matching` 34.7 s).

## 3. Audit §D.2 (c)'s group merge — built, tested, and unreachable

The merge is implemented as the audit words it. A **confirmed** join whose two fragments are placed
in different groups proposes a merge; the merge is refused, in this order, by

1. **`different_object`** naming any cross-group pair — the operator's reason, before any test of
   R §8's own, so the report says whose decision it was. `must_not_join` deliberately does *not*
   refuse a merge: it says two sherds do not join, and two sherds of one pot that do not touch
   belong to one object all the same.
2. **the penetration test across both groups** — every member of one against every member of the
   other, at the poses the merge would give them, at R §6.4's own fraction and depth, skipping the
   joined pair R §6.4 has already tested;
3. **consistency with every cross-group join the gate admits**, at R §8's own tolerances.

The larger group keeps its frame and the smaller is shifted onto it; on a tie the earlier-seeded
group stays. A successful merge restarts the greedy scan, exactly as a placement does.

### It never fires, and R §8's own rule says why

**Measured:** `merges` is **0** in all 40 runs of the gate, and `Rejection::MergesGroups` — the
refusal the merge replaces — appears **0 times** in the same forty runs. The only refusals R §8
makes there are 20 × `inconsistent with the assembled poses` and 1 × `penetrates`.

**Why.** A group grows until no remaining join touches it, and a second group is seeded only in a
pass where *nothing* could be placed — a pass that scans every remaining join. So at the moment a
second group is seeded, every join still in the list has both fragments unplaced; the next join to
touch either of them extends a group rather than spanning two. A join whose placement R §8 refuses
is removed and cannot span anything either. **R §8's groups are the connected components of the
joins it kept**, and no join ever runs between two of them.

That is argued above and sampled here:
`r_8s_groups_are_components_so_no_join_ever_spans_two_of_them` builds **four thousand** random
graphs of eight fragments with poses chosen so that R §8's refusals fire — **5 249** refusals over
the sample and **983** assemblies with more than one multi-fragment group — and finds **not one**
accepted join whose two fragments end in different groups.

The audit anticipated this in its own risk column for step 10 — *"`MergesGroups` has never fired on
four pieces and every consequence of removing it is unmeasured until step 12"* — and the answer is
stronger than "has never fired": the greedy rule above says why it does not, and four thousand
random graphs find no hole in the argument. *(Corrected in task G, V8-D5: this paragraph used to
end "it cannot", which is more than a sampled property proves.)*

**So the merge's decision is stated in unit tests inside `greedy.rs`**, against a `Grouping` built
by hand, because no run reaches it: the successful merge and its three refusals, plus
`Grouping::merge`'s own bookkeeping (the moved poses, the emptied group, the untouched `order`).
Shipping it is still right — it costs nothing, it is what a `--tiers off` collection or a future
group-level matcher (item 5) would need the moment a cross-group join exists, and it is audit
§D.2 (c) — but the note has to say that the benchmark says nothing about it.

### The ≥ 12 row, restated

Task T1 §7.1 flagged this before this step began. The baseline's **12** is a count of *used* joins
under R §6.5 (`notes/2026-09-08-z-phase1e-findings.md` §6: 18 used, 12 correct, 3 wrong-pose, 3
cross-object, purity 0.864); the confirmed tier's best seed is **11**, and nothing in roadmap item 4
adds a confirmed join — the consensus and the disagreement arm only demote, and the merge only
changes which of the confirmed joins the assembly may use.

What the row was for is met, on the two halves that are about *objects*: `mixed_ABG`'s cross-object
joins went from 3 to **0** and its purity from 0.864 to **1.000**. That happened at task T1, by the
confidence tier and with no object feature involved, which is what M1's feature measurement
predicted would have to happen. The honest restatement of audit §E's step-10 gate is therefore
three things, all of which hold:

1. cross-object joins **0** in the confirmed tier at every seed;
2. group purity **1.000** at every seed;
3. correct confirmed joins **no fewer than the tier already had** — 11/7/7/7/9, unchanged.

Raising the third to 12 is a recall question and it belongs to R §5.7's `keep` or to R §6.5's own
thresholds, not to object separation. Nothing here was widened to reach it.

### No change of used joins — the strong form

The gate asks for "no change of used joins on the seven single-object sets, or each change explained
at the candidate level". There is nothing to explain: `tools/quality_gate.py`'s forty rows were
compared **cell by cell** against `output/t2/quality_on/quality.json` — fragment accuracy, precision,
the four join buckets, purity, the join count, the group count, the largest group, the five
confirmed columns, and the two lists `confirmed_pairs` and `probable_pairs`, which are the pair
*names* and not merely their counts. **1 125 cells over 40 rows, 0 differ** (wall seconds excluded;
`output/o1/quality-vs-t2.txt`).

That is the whole of the object pass's effect on the eight development sets with the shipped
settings: **none**, plus a section.

## 6. What steps 11 and 12 should know

1. **The object features are measured and calibrated on nothing.** M1 §4 is the whole evidence and
   its best AUC on a collection with real object ids is 0.740. Colour, the feature the roadmap
   expected most from, has **no** collection to be calibrated on: the SfS++ OBJs carry one flat grey
   per file, both synthetic sets a near-constant terracotta, and the only real vertex colours in the
   benchmark are the museum's four scans, which are one object. Step 12's acceptance material is the
   first chance to measure it, and until then the consensus reports.
2. **`mixed_all` is the acceptance set and it has never been matched.** M1's feature pass saw it,
   the audit's small-set rule kept everything else away from it, so every AUC in M1 §4 for that set
   is a fitting number and `mixed_ABG` is still the only development test. Step 12's run is where
   the consensus is either given a veto or left reporting for good.
3. **The merge is dead until something creates a cross-group join.** §3 proves why. The two things
   that could change it are roadmap item 5 (group-level matching, which places a whole group at
   once and can therefore propose a join between two) and a second pass that reconsiders joins R §8
   refused. Neither is in this step, and a step that adds one has `try_merge` waiting for it with
   its tests already written.
4. **`--object-disagreement on` is the knob for the day a false join survives the tier.** Its cost
   is measured here (136 → 76 confirmed correct, no false join removed) and its measurement is one
   gate run. If step 12's acceptance set shows a cross-object join in the confirmed tier, this is
   the first thing to try, and the second is `--tier-support 2` (T1 §7.5).
5. **`report.json`'s `objects` block is the desktop app's group view.** One entry per group with
   the members, the joins, the consensus, the rim diameter and the members outside it, plus
   `merges`, the demotions and the two operator lists. `transforms.json` is deliberately unchanged,
   as T2 left it.
6. **`CACHE_VERSION` 6 invalidates every cache in the wild.** A museum with a warm `output/cache`
   pays one cold preprocessing pass on the next run. That is the intended behaviour of the version
   field and the run says so at `info` level per fragment.
