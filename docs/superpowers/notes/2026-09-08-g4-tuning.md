# G4 — phase 2e: tuning what a measurement justifies, and what `info` should say about it

**Date:** 2026-09-08. Branch `rust-core`, from `d94f2fb` (the head of task G3).
Design references: D §1 (the device budget), §5 (threading), §6.4 (batch formation and the
software pipeline), §6.6 (expected speedups), §6.8 (operational concerns), §9 (the CLI), §10.3
(benchmark gates), §12 (phasing). Experiments E7 (wgpu on Metal) and E8 (the vendor matrix), tasks
G1 (the device), G2 (the kernels) and G3 (the pipeline). Machine: Apple M2 Pro, 10 cores (6P+4E),
16-core GPU, 16 GB, **one Metal adapter and no software fallback**.

**Verdict: two threshold changes, measured, worth 1.10× of the matching stage together on
`synthetic_20` and nothing on the small collections, where neither can fire. And the gain task G3
left on the table — `--gpu-memory 0.05`'s 1.37× — turns out not to be a gain that can be taken:
the measurement in §2 shows that flag was refusing batches by *when they arrived*, not by how big
they are, which is a rule that would make two runs of the same collection disagree.**

The stage over the seven development collections is now **1.07–1.43×** against G3's 1.04–1.40×, and
`synthetic_20` — the one collection here where the device has real work to do — moves **1.08 →
1.28×**. Every D §10.3 runtime row is inside its gate on both backends, warm and cold. Nothing
moved: parity is 23 804 / 0, and every used join, group and pose on all seven collections is
bit-identical to G3's **on both backends**, although the new ceiling took 41 of `synthetic_20`'s
190 coarse calls off the device.

`AUTO_ELIGIBLE` stays `false`. D §6.8's bar is 1.5× of the stage and the honest measurement is
1.07–1.43×.

---

## 1. How these numbers were taken, and what they are not

Every comparison below is **interleaved within a round** — `cpu, base, variant₁, variant₂, …`,
then the same again — and reported as the **median of the per-round paired ratios**, not as a
ratio of medians. That is not pedantry on this machine: over one eight-round session the CPU
control alone moved between 15.10 s and 21.42 s for the identical run, because the machine is a
laptop with a browser and a VPN client on it and because half an hour of continuous benchmarking
warms an M2 Pro. A ratio of medians across a session like that measures the session; a median of
paired ratios does not.

The noise floor was measured rather than assumed, and the way it was measured is worth keeping.
In the seven-cell sweep of §5, two of the cells on `pot_H` — `ceil` and `base` — are provably the
**same configuration**, because `pot_H`'s largest coarse batch is 3.6 M queries and the ceiling
being tested is 12 M, so not one call changes hands (the `coarse: … on device` counters in both
logs read 55). They measured 1.039× apart. So on `pot_H`, at four rounds, **4 % is zero**, and
nothing smaller than about 10 % is a result there. `synthetic_20` is quieter — its stage is twice
as long — but not much: single-round ratios there range over ±12 % around their median.

All runs are warm-cache, `--no-preview --no-meshes`, `--threads` at its default nine, and quote
the **matching stage** rather than the wall, because that is the only stage the backend touches.
The logs are under `output/bench_g4/`.

## 2. The gain that was not there: what `--gpu-memory 0.05` was actually doing

Task G3 §8 measured something striking and left it as this task's first item: capping the device
budget at 50 MB refused seventeen of `synthetic_20`'s 326 device batches and took the matching
stage from 13.07 s to 11.16 s, **1.37× the CPU backend against 1.17× uncapped**. G3 read it as a
size effect — *"refusing the seventeen largest batches"* — and D §6.6 wrote it down as "the
cheapest remaining gain in the GPU path".

It is not a size effect. Every call of a warm `synthetic_20` run was traced through
`Allocations::reserve` — the bytes each call asks for and the bytes already live when it asks:

| | request | live at the request |
|---|---|---|
| minimum | 0.34 MB | — |
| median | 3.98 MB | 25.7 MB |
| p90 | 11.88 MB | 63.3 MB |
| **maximum** | **20.73 MB** | **84.7 MB** |
| peak allocation over the run | — | 100 MB |

**The largest single batch on the device is a fifth of the peak.** The peak is five concurrent
calls, not one big one; and a 50 MB budget therefore refuses a call not because *it* is large but
because *four others were already running*. That is a property of the schedule — of which worker
reached `reserve` first — and not of the batch.

Which makes it unusable, for a reason that has nothing to do with taste. G2 measured the coarse
kernel as bit-identical to the CPU executor on 2 934 052 of 2 934 122 hypotheses and **one probe
point away on the other 70**, so *which* calls run on the device is visible in the last bits of the
output. A rule that depends on arrival order would put a different set of calls on the device in
every run, and D §7's gate — two runs of the same collection, byte-identical outputs — would stop
holding. G3's own check ("the joins are unchanged at 20 MB") is a weaker statement than that gate
and does not rescue it.

So the honest form of "there is a ceiling" is a ceiling that is **a function of the batch**, which
is what §3 measures. And the 1.37× is not a number this task can inherit: it was measured with a
rule the port cannot adopt.

## 3. Change 1 — `coarse::MAX_QUERIES`, a ceiling beside the floor

### 3.1 What the batches look like

Traced over a warm `synthetic_20` (`--backend gpu`, the calls that pass the existing floors):

| method | on-device calls | work | median | maximum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `coarse_scores` | 192 | 1.65 G point-queries | 8.1 M | **22.7 M** |
| `icp_rung` | 134 | 17.9 M candidate-points | 0.14 M | **0.27 M** |

Two things follow immediately. The coarse batches span two orders of magnitude and reach the
dispatch cap, so a ceiling has something to act on. The ICP batches do not: 0.27 M candidate-points
is three orders below anything a ceiling would refuse, the candidate counts are 97–250 against
D §6.4's 512 dispatch bound, and `icp::MAX_CANDIDATES` never binds on these collections. **There is
no ICP ceiling in this task, because there is nothing for one to do.**

The coarse work is also almost entirely R §5.2's own batch: 190 of the 192 calls are at sixty probe
points, and the two `brk_sub` re-scores of R §5.4 that reach the device carry 0.2 and 0.3 M queries
between them.

### 3.2 The sweep

Three warm runs at each ceiling, interleaved with a CPU control, matching-stage medians:

| ceiling | calls refused of 190 | queries left on the device | matching | against the CPU | device outstanding |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 M | 165 | 4 % | 15.19 s | 1.03× | 2.3–2.6 s |
| 6 M | 132 | 14 % | 14.11 s | 1.11× | 2.8–3.0 s |
| 8 M | 98 | 29 % | 13.03 s | 1.20× | 3.4–3.8 s |
| **12 M** | **41** | **62 %** | **12.16 s** | **1.29×** | 5.4–5.9 s |
| 16 M | 12 | 87 % | 12.23 s | 1.28× | 7.5–8.9 s |
| none | 0 | 100 % | 12.78 s | 1.23× | 10.2–10.9 s |

The curve has a broad minimum between 12 M and 16 M and falls away below 8 M, so the constant is
**12 M**, the middle of the flat part rather than the best single median.

Two things in that table are worth more than the wall-clock column. First, moving 38 % of the
device's work to the CPU **halves the device's occupancy and leaves the stage where it was** —
10.2 s of outstanding device time becomes 5.8 s while the stage moves 12.78 → 12.16 s. The two
sides of this machine are close to interchangeable on this workload, which is D §6.6's envelope
stated as an equivalence rather than as a complaint. Second, the direction is the opposite of what
the *idle*-device crossover in `tests/adapter.rs` says: there, the largest cell in the table
(40 000 poses × 800 points, 32 M queries) is the **best** one at 3.6×. A ceiling could never have
been derived from that test, and the test now says so in its own documentation.

### 3.3 What it does on the other collections

Nothing, and provably. `pot_H`'s 55 coarse calls carry 0.10 G queries with a largest batch of
**3.6 M**; the other five development collections are smaller still. The ceiling is a threshold
for the collections whose batches are large — `synthetic_20` among the development sets, and by
extension the three large collections that are not run until the final acceptance — and on the
sherd sets it never fires. That is why §5's table shows it as exactly zero there.

## 4. Change 2 — the slack stops at the machine

G3 measured `Executor::device_slack` at `--threads` 1, 4 and 9 and found 1.93×, 1.36× and 1.00×.
It read the last row as *free*. It is not free; it is the top of a hill. Sweeping the slack alone
at the default `--threads 9` (three warm runs each, `synthetic_20`, matching-stage medians):

| slack | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | **5 — G3's** | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pool | 9 | 10 | **11** | 12 | 14 | 17 |
| matching | 12.84 s | 12.43 s | **12.03 s** | 12.87 s | 13.80 s | 14.05 s |
| device outstanding | 8.3 s | 8.2 s | 8.4 s | 9.3 s | 9.7 s | 10.0 s |

The minimum is broad — 10 to 12 workers are inside the run-to-run spread of each other — and 14
and 17 are outside it. The mechanism is G3 §5's envelope read from the pool's side: a worker
waiting on the device is not using a core, so a few extra workers cost nothing and cover the wait;
past one worker per core, the extra ones are not covering a wait at all, they are putting another
concurrent batch on a device that gets slower the busier the cores beside it are — which the
"device outstanding" row shows directly, rising 8.3 → 10.0 s for the same work.

So the fraction stays and the result is capped:

```
device_slack = min(⌈threads / 2⌉, cores + 1 − threads), never below 1
```

*one worker per core, plus one that may be waiting on the device.* It reproduces every row G3
measured — `--threads 1` still gets a pool of 2 and `--threads 4` a pool of 6 on this ten-core
machine, which are the rows where the slack was worth 1.93× and 1.36× — and takes the default from
14 workers to 11, which is where the table above has its minimum. `--threads` above the core count
is the caller's decision and still gets its one waiter.

## 5. The two together, and the 3 % bar

Four interleaved rounds of seven cells, `--threads 9` throughout, median of the per-round paired
ratios against `base` (= G3's defaults):

| | `synthetic_20` | `pot_H` |
|---|---|---|
| ceiling alone | **1.020×** | 1.039× — *the same configuration as `base`*, i.e. the noise floor |
| pool alone (11) | **1.024×** | 1.058× (noise) |
| pool alone (10) | 0.974× | 1.091× (noise) |
| **both (ceiling + pool 11)** | **1.071×** | 1.038× (noise) |

and then eight further interleaved rounds of `cpu, base, both`:

| | `synthetic_20` | `pot_H` |
|---|---|---|
| `both` against `base`, median of 8 paired ratios | **1.106×** | 1.014× |
| rounds favouring `both` | 6 of 8 | 4 of 8 |

Pooling the twelve rounds of both experiments, `synthetic_20` reads **1.10×** with 9 of 12 rounds
favouring the change, and `pot_H` reads **1.01×**, which on a set with a 4 % noise floor and a
ceiling that provably never fires is the right answer.

**The task's rule was to keep a change only if it is worth ≥ 3 % of matching wall, and the honest
reading is that neither change clears that bar alone (2.0 % and 2.4 %) while the pair clears it
comfortably (10 %).** They are not independent: both move work off the device and back onto the
ten cores, and the envelope they are both fighting is one resource, so a partial move gets a
partial answer. The decision is to keep both, because the pair is the change and dropping either
one costs most of it — and to say so here rather than quote 10 % against a threshold that was met
by a combination.

## 6. What else was looked at, and why it was not changed

The task's list of candidates was *"workgroup sizes, candidates per dispatch, pairs per batch, grid
cell size vs radius, slot reuse order, upload/readback batching"*. Four of the six are answered by
measurements that already exist, and answering them is cheaper than sweeping them.

**Candidates per dispatch.** `icp::MAX_CANDIDATES` is D §6.4's 512, for a Windows TDR watchdog. The
ICP batches that reach the device on these collections hold **97 to 250** candidates, so the
constant never binds and there is nothing to tune: raising it changes nothing and lowering it would
only split batches that are not being split. `synthetic_20`'s 134 on-device rungs are one dispatch
each.

**Upload/readback batching and slot reuse.** G3 §2 profiled a GPU-backend run and found the host
side of a dispatch — building the hash grid, interleaving the clouds, creating the buffers and the
bind groups, reading the results back — at **1.31 of 78.2 core-seconds, 1.7 %**. That is the
ceiling on everything a residency cache, a slot table or a batched readback could return, and it is
below this machine's noise floor. `SlotTable` still has no consumer for the reason G3 §8 gives: the
kernels upload a *pair's* target at a *rung's* radius, not a fragment, so there is no per-fragment
object to keep resident.

**Grid cell size against the radius.** The cell is the radius, and that is not a free parameter:
`nearest_below` visits the 3×3×3 block around the query's cell, which covers the radius ball
exactly when the cell is at least the radius, and a cell of *k* radii keeps the same 27 probes
while multiplying the points inside each of them by *k³*. Smaller than the radius does not cover
the ball at all. The structure is also `sherd-core`'s, shared with the CPU executor and with the
parity gate that says the CPU path has not moved, so it is not a place to put a GPU tuning
parameter.

**Pairs per batch.** This is D §6.5's multi-pair batch, the last structural idea in the design that
has not been tried, and it is a rewrite of `matching::pair` into phases across pairs rather than a
threshold. G3 §11.4 asked for a projection of what it is worth before it is started, and §8 below
is now that projection: the device executes 24.8 ms per pair and the stage costs 63.7 ms per pair,
so a perfect multi-pair batch — one that eliminated every per-call upload and every wait — could
not take more than the difference, and §5's envelope says a large part of that difference is the
cores and the device slowing each other down rather than either of them waiting.

**Workgroup width — swept, and the answer is "not measurable here".** `coarse.wgsl` puts one
workgroup on one hypothesis with 64 lanes striding over the probe, which at R §5.2's sixty probe
points is one query per lane followed by a six-round reduction tree. Halving the width doubles the
queries per lane and removes a round; doubling it does the opposite. The tally is `u32` and integer
addition is associative, so **the width cannot move a single score** — it is a pure timing knob,
which is what makes it worth sweeping at all.

Three binaries were built and then interleaved (`64, 32, 128` each round), nine rounds on
`synthetic_20`, paired ratios against 64:

| | ratios, sorted | median | rounds favouring it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32 lanes | 0.905 0.909 0.951 0.963 1.051 1.080 1.099 1.204 1.250 | 1.051× | **5 of 9** |
| 128 lanes | 0.846 0.924 0.973 1.018 1.023 1.028 1.032 1.202 1.203 | 1.023× | 6 of 9 |

A median of 1.05× whose sign flips in four rounds of nine is not a 5 % result, it is a coin flip
with a wide distribution, and the width stays at **64**. What would settle it is a measurement of
the kernel rather than of the stage — but §3.2 is the reason not to take one on an idle device,
where this workload behaves the opposite way round. It is left where it is, with the numbers, and
`pipeline::DEPTH` beside it in §11.

E7 §8 asked for this section to be redone from measured throughput rather than from the assumption
it was written with. Every figure below is from a `--threads 1` run, where the submitter has one
command buffer outstanding at a time and `Occupancy` is device *execution* rather than execution

The assumption is **1.4–2.8× optimistic** on the workload it is about, which is the correction E7

## 9. Item 3 — E8's vendor matrix, stated honestly

D §12 puts a vendor matrix (NVIDIA / AMD / Intel / Apple) in phase 2e. **It cannot be run here, and
no part of this task pretends otherwise.** This machine exposes exactly one adapter — Metal on an
Apple M2 Pro — and macOS ships no software rasteriser for wgpu to fall back on (E7 §6), so a
failing self-test here has no second opinion and a second vendor has no representative.

What CI does check, on the four platforms of D §10.5, without a GPU:

* the workspace **builds**, including `sherd-gpu`, with and without the `gpu` feature;
* every test that does not need an adapter runs: the chunking arithmetic, the 2-D dispatch fold,
  the buffer size arithmetic, the reduction mirror, `Selection::decide`'s rule, the two thresholds
  of §3 and §4, and the batch-formation unit tests;
* every adapter-dependent test **skips with a printed reason** rather than passing silently —
  `SKIP …: no adapter`, which is visible in the CI log.

What CI cannot check, and what would therefore still be unknown after a green run on four
platforms:

* that the kernels produce the same answers on any driver but this one — the reduction's
  bit-identity and the bounded-NN tie rate are *measurements of Metal*, and E7 §3–§5 say so;
* the discrete-GPU staging path, because there is no discrete GPU here (E7 §6);
* **whether §3's ceiling and §4's slack cap are the right numbers anywhere else at all.** Both are
  consequences of an *integrated* part sharing one power and bandwidth budget with the CPU. A
  discrete card does not share it, would not lose 1.6× of its throughput as the cores fill up, and
  would very likely want no ceiling and a deeper pool. They are measured constants for this class
  of machine, and the honest form of the vendor matrix is that each row of it has to re-measure
  them rather than inherit them.

## 11. What is left

1. **The envelope is still the ceiling.** 1.07–1.43× is what this task's thresholds get out of an
   integrated GPU that shares power and bandwidth with ten busy cores. D §6.8's bar is 1.5× and
   `AUTO_ELIGIBLE` stays `false`. The measurement that would change the picture is E8's discrete
   rows, which §9 says cannot be taken here.
2. **`--threads 6` is no longer the best GPU configuration, and nothing was measured to replace
   it.** G3's minimum at six workers was a minimum of *pool depth*, and §4 now sets the pool depth
   from the machine rather than from `--threads`, so the two knobs no longer say the same thing.
   What a later phase would want is what G3 §11.2 already asked for: a self-test that times the
   stage's own shape rather than a bounded-NN kernel.
3. **`pipeline::DEPTH` was not re-measured after the pool changed.** G3 chose 4 over 2 and 8 with
   the pool at 14; the pool is now 11, and the two interact — the depth is how many submissions may
   be outstanding and the pool is how many workers can be waiting for them. It is a compile-time
   constant, so measuring it means three binaries interleaved the way §6 measures the workgroup
   width, and it is the cheapest remaining experiment in the GPU path.
4. **The multi-pair batch of D §6.5 is still the last structural idea untried**, and §6 now carries
   the projection G3 §11.4 asked for before it is started: 24.8 ms of device execution inside a
   63.7 ms pair.
5. **`gpu-check`'s stage-2 tail is G2's and is unchanged**, to the digit. It changes no join on any
   development set; G2 §5 separates the ladder's share from the kernel's.
