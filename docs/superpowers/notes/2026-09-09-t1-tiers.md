# T1: confidence tiers — the band, the evidence, and the assembly built from it

**Date:** 2026-09-09. **Tree:** branch `rust-core`, previous step `218b5af` (M1.1).
**Step:** 8 of the audit's amended plan (`notes/2026-09-09-fable-audit.md` §E; D §12 row 8).
**What it is:** roadmap item 3's confidence tier. Every accepted candidate is probed once more and
lands in one of three bands; **R §8 assembles from the confirmed band and from nothing else**;
`report.md` gains the three bands with their evidence and a per-fragment index; `transforms.json`
and `report.json` carry the band per join and the set it was decided with. `--tiers off` reproduces
the previous step's outputs byte for byte.

Every threshold here was chosen in task M1 (`notes/2026-09-09-m1-measure.md`) on 2 916 measured
candidates before a line of this step was written. Nothing was re-fitted.

## 1. The tier

```
Confirmed = tight  >= 0.35
        AND gap    <= 0.015 t
        AND seam   >= 5 t
        AND cont_n >= 0.90
        AND pen    <= 0
        AND slide  <= 0.1 t
        AND ( support >= 1  OR  margin >= 2 over a second placement that scores )
Probable  = R §6.5 accepted it and the line above did not confirm it
Rejected  = R §6.5 refused it, with the reason it has today
```

M1 §3's chosen set, value for value, and every value is a flag (`--tier-min-tight`,
`--tier-max-gap`, `--tier-min-seam`, `--tier-min-cont-n`, `--tier-max-pen`, `--tier-max-slide`,
`--tier-margin`, `--tier-support`). Audit §D.1 names two further arms and M1 measured that neither
can do the work asked of it, so both are `Option`s that default to **off** and are flags all the
same:

* `--tier-max-determined-deg` — determinedness separates (AUC 0.879) and cannot be thresholded:
  the whole measured range over 808 candidates is 1.6e-14 to 4.1e-7 degrees. It is **reported** in
  every band's evidence and read by nothing.
* `--tier-resample-accept` — requiring all three draws to accept costs six confirmed joins
  (136 → 130) and removes no false one. The three draws are reported as `redraws n/3`.

**The support count is in the tier and audit §D.2 put it in step 10.** M1's reason, restated
because it is the one thing about this step that departs from the audit's plan: no conjunction of
the five scores, the margin, the slide, determinedness and the redraws reaches zero false joins —
4 478 976 combinations were searched — and the survivors are one family of wrong-pose joins on
genuinely adjacent pairs (pot_H 02-04, 02-11, 03-04, 03-07, 09-11 and pot_A 02-05) that are
indistinguishable from true joins on every quantity R §6 measures. What refuses them is that no
third fragment agrees with where they put the sherd. Without the support count the highest-recall
zero-false rule is `gap <= 0.0064 t`, whose nearest false join sits 0.5 % of a boundary away — a
fit to forty runs, not a threshold.

**Audit §D.2 words the support arm as "two consistent independent paths" and this ships one.**
M1 measured why: **every one** of the 466 false joins has a support count of zero, and only 92 of
the 342 correct ones reach one or more. At two the arm fires on almost nothing and the recall it
buys is gone; at one it is a one-sided certificate with no counterexample in the forty runs.
`--tier-support 2` is the flag that asks for the audit's own wording.

**The margin is read strictly.** A pair whose kept list holds one placement *fails* the margin test
rather than passing it with an infinite margin. That is M1 §3's finding and not a choice made here:
read the other way the test is inert — `strict ∧ gap ≤ 0.015 t ∧ slide ≤ 0.1 t` confirms 293
correct and 18 false, and adding a loose `margin ≥ 2` to it removes neither a correct join nor a
false one.

### Where the pass runs, and what it costs

After matching and **before** R §8, because R §8 assembles from its answer. It needs nothing the
assembly produces: the support count walks the accepted-candidate graph — for every third fragment
`x` with accepted joins to both, `T_ax · T_xb` is an independent opinion about where B goes —
rather than the groups, which is exactly what makes a band decidable before anything is placed. It
needs R §6.1's fracture BVHs, which are alive between the last pair and the block that releases
them.

R §8.1's second pass rebuilds the candidate list, so the band is recomputed rather than patched:
the margin, the placement count and the support count are statements about the list as a whole and
a pair rematched with the larger budget changes them for pairs it never touched. The second pass is
off by default.

## 2. What a candidate's band is decided on, and where each probe restarts

The five probes are M1's, line for line — `measure.rs` and `tiers.rs` now share one implementation
so that a threshold cannot come to mean something else than it was chosen on. Two of them re-climb
R §5.6's ladder and both restart from the candidate's **own final pose**.

| probe | what it asks | where it restarts |
|---|---|---|
| `margin` | how far ahead of the pair's second *placement* is this one? | no ladder; the pair's kept list, clustered at one wall (`SAME_PLACEMENT_T`, the parity harness's own constant, measured its way) |
| `determined_deg` / `determined_t` | is the pose a function of its input? | the twelve one-ULP neighbours of the pose through R §5.6's **last two rungs**, which is the audit's own wording |
| `slide_t` | pushed half a wall along the seam, does the fit come back? | ±0.5 t along the seam's principal axis, then **the whole of R §5.6** — the last fracture rung alone has a 0.04 t radius against a 0.5 t push and cannot answer (M1 §0) |
| `resample_*` | do the scores survive two independent redraws of `Pf`, `S` and the margin? | no ladder; R §6 again at the same pose on two redrawn collections (`seed + 1e6`, `seed + 2e6`) |
| `support` | how many independent accepted joins agree with this placement? | no geometry; R §8's tolerances (10°, 0.5 t) read **at B's fracture cloud** and not at the file origin, which on these scans is a lever arm of 100–500 units |

Every one of them is reported for every accepted candidate, whether or not the tier reads it: the
probable list is what a conservator works from, and the numbers behind a refusal are what makes it
arguable.

### One design question the measurement answered rather than the code

A pair can have several accepted candidates and the tier is computed per candidate, so a pair whose
*best* candidate is only probable could in principle have a lower-ranked one confirmed. Over the
forty runs of M1's dumps it never happens: the confirmed pair set is the same 136 whether the band
is read on the best accepted candidate of each pair or on any of them, **0 extra pairs and 0 false
joins either way**. The implementation takes the per-candidate reading — it is the honest one, and
R §8's `best_per_pair` then picks the best *confirmed* candidate of a pair rather than the best
accepted one.

## 3. What the outputs say

### `report.md`

Four new sections, in the order a bench reads them, and each one absent from a `--tiers off` run:

* **`## Confirmed joins`** — the tier's own sentence in plain language followed by one row per
  confirmed pair: the five scores, the slide, the margin, the support count, the placement count,
  the redraws and the determinedness. The assembly above it is built from these.
* **`## Probable joins`** — R §6.5 accepted them and the tier did not confirm them. Same columns
  plus **why not confirmed**, which names the test and the number: `tight 0.2539 < 0.35`,
  `neither arm: support 0 < 1 and no second placement to beat`. They are listed and never placed.
* **`## Rejected`** — how many pairs R §6.5 refused and which limit each failed, as a tally, then
  the twenty-five highest-scoring with the sentence R §6.5 has for them
  (`gap 0.0572 t > 0.0300 t; penetration 0.0089 > 0.005`). A 24-fragment collection refuses two
  hundred pairs and nobody reads two hundred rows; R §11.3's own **Best candidate per pair**, which
  this report still writes in full, is where the rest are.
* **`## Candidates by fragment`** — audit §C.8(a): *"the most useful output for a bench is not the
  assembly but, per fragment, its best two or three candidate partners with pictures and numbers"*.
  Every fragment with the partners R §6.5 accepted for it, best band first and best score inside a
  band, each row carrying the band, the scores, the slide, the margin, the support and the refusal.
  A pair appears once under each of its two fragments.

`## Accepted joins not used` — R §8's own refusals — keeps its name with the tier off and reads
`## Confirmed joins not used` with it on, because that is what it then holds.

### `report.json` and `transforms.json`

* `params.tiers` — **the tier set used**, in both files. It is the 47th key of `Params` and it is
  skipped when the pass is off, which is what keeps a `--tiers off` run's two files byte-identical
  to the reference's.
* `report.json`: `tier` and `evidence` on every entry of `candidates`, `joins_used` and
  `joins_rejected`; a `tiers` block with the three counts **over pairs** (several candidates of one
  pair routinely converge on one placement, so the candidate-level count is not the number of
  joins).
* `transforms.json`: `tiers`, one entry per pair that produced a candidate — `a`, `b`, `tier`,
  `score`. A downstream tool holding the poses has the band beside them without opening the report.

## 4. The gate

`tools/quality_gate.py` gains `--tiers on|off` and, with the tier on, four columns and one
prohibition.

* The **left half** is what it always was — `evaluate.py` over the joins R §8 *used*, which under
  `--tiers on` are confirmed joins.
* The **right half** is the confirmed tier itself: every confirmed pair of the run, classified by
  `evaluate.py`'s own rule applied to that candidate's **own** pose. That is the set audit §D.1
  states the prohibition over, and it is not the same set as the assembly: a confirmed join R §8
  refused for penetration or for merging two groups still has to be a true join, and here it is
  scored. On `mixed_ABG` seed 0 the assembly used 8 of the 11 confirmed joins, so gating the
  assembly instead of the tier would have left three joins unexamined.
* The **prohibition** is on all eight sets, `mixed_ABG` included: **zero wrong-pose, non-adjacent
  and cross-object joins in the confirmed tier**, at every seed. Confirmed recall is reported per
  set beside it.
* The **band** is not compared under `--tiers on`. R §13's bands are the *reference's* five
  accepted-tier draws; an assembly of confirmed joins is a different quantity and reporting the
  difference would report the tier working. `--tiers off` still makes the comparison.

### Audit §E's step-8 terracotta gate, restated

Audit §E words it "terracotta's two joins confirmed". M1 §5.6 measured, before this step, that the

## 7. What steps 9 and 10 should know

1. **Item 4's own gate is already met on the development test, and not by item 4.** `mixed_ABG`'s
   confirmed tier is cross-object 0 and purity 1.000 at every seed, with 7–11 correct joins — audit
   §E's step-10 gate asks for "cross-object 0 in the confirmed tier, purity 1.000, correct joins
   ≥ 12 (the baseline's)". The first two hold now; **the third does not** — the baseline's 12 is a
   count of *used* joins under R §6.5 and the confirmed tier's best seed is 11. Step 10 should
   restate that row against the tier it is now gating, or accept the recall it inherits.
2. **The support count is now load-bearing and its own weakness is measured.** It fires on 92 of
   342 correct joins and on none of the 466 false ones, and it can never fire on a chain of three
   (the terracotta) or on a collection with one accepted join. Step 10's group merging and its
   mutual-disagreement demotion (audit §D.2 (b), (c)) act on the same graph and should be built
   beside `tiers::probe`'s `support_count`, not beside a second implementation of it.
3. **No object feature may demote.** M1 §4 measured every feature on every collection with real
   object ids and the best AUC is 0.740, under audit §D.2's own 0.800 rule. `GroupFeatures` reports
